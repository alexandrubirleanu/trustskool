# Skool Live Calls V2 Is Here, Plus a Growth Boost Algorithm Built Around Retention

Skool News #82 announces that Live Calls V2 has shipped to everyone, previews a wishlist feature for saving groups, and explains the next Growth Boost algorithm, which will reward communities whose members keep paying a year after joining.

## Live Calls V2: same features, new foundation

The new live calls are meant to look and work almost exactly like the old ones. Calls and webinars, settings and menus are basically unchanged. What changed is the infrastructure underneath. The hosts say the old version was more convenient than Zoom or Google Meet but not as stable. Problems included heavy lag, dropped audio, people getting kicked out, needing a VPN in some countries such as Dubai or China, a chat with a very short character limit, and blurry small text when screen sharing, which hurt traders sharing charts.

The goal of the rebuild was to match Zoom and Google Meet on quality and performance. A group of users ran it for a couple of weeks before launch and reported better quality. In one example the hosts were careful not to over-read, a creator's daily live attendance roughly doubled, from about 250 to 600, with nothing changed except the infrastructure. Their theory is that people roll in and out of lives, and better quality makes them more likely to stay.

V2 is rolling out to everyone. Calls already in progress stay on the old system until they end, and the next call starts on the new one. The hosts expect some bugs and ask owners to report them in the Skoolers community.

## Recently shipped

The hosts recap three launches: Global Feed, which they say is more popular than expected; Discovery categories expanded from 9 to around 30; and Live Calls V2.

## Coming soon: save to wishlist

Skool has started work on a save button for communities you like but are not ready to join, similar to wishlists on shopping sites. Saved groups would appear in a wishlist. The hosts add that saves could also be a signal Growth Boost uses to promote a group to the people who saved it.

## The algorithm shift: volume, value, then retention

Growth Boost is only about three or four months old, and its algorithm has changed as Skool learns:

- **V1** had a bias for volume, meaning frequent sales. It favored low-ticket groups and largely neglected mid and higher-ticket ones.
- **V2**, the current version, has a bias for purchase value, so more $99 to $300 groups get promoted, with the average settling around $100 a month.
- **V3**, which the hosts say will start shipping within about a week across ads, Discovery, browse, search and suggestions, has a bias for retention.

The data showed some low-ticket groups were more profitable to promote than expensive ones. A $9 group with a great experience can lead members to buy several more groups and stay active for a year, while a $200 group might churn people after a month or two. V3 projects what share of a group's members will still be active and paying one year after joining, and the higher that projection, the more Growth Boost a group gets. It still factors in volume and value, so both low and mid-ticket groups can win if retention is strong.

## Skool of the week

This week featured smaller niche groups: a mini pig hoof care community at $16 a month making about $500 a month, an Iceland travel planning club making about $420 a month, and a dragon fruit growing community at $17 a month making about $1,600 a month.

## Why this matters for people evaluating Skool communities

The hosts note that retention is highly correlated with quality: when they sorted groups by retention, the best-retaining ones were simply better products. If Skool's own promotion is moving toward rewarding long-term paying members, then retention is the number that tells you most about whether a community is worth joining, and the hardest one to fake.
