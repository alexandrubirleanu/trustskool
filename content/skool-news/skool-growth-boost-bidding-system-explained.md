# Skool Adds a Growth Boost Bidding System (Skool News #71)

Episode #71 returns to Growth Boost, Skool's platform-funded advertising feature, with a change to how owners control how much reach they get: a bidding system that lets a community set how aggressively it competes for ad placement.

## How the bid works

Instead of a flat, one-size-fits-all boost, owners now get a slider to set their bid as a percentage, effectively how much they're willing to put behind Growth Boost relative to other communities competing for the same ad inventory. A higher bid means more aggressive placement and, presumably, more of the traffic Growth Boost is capable of driving; a lower bid means a lighter footprint. Critically, the bid only applies to acquiring new members going forward, it has no effect on a community's existing member base, pricing, or anyone already signed up. It's a dial on future growth spend, not a lever that touches current operations.

## Why a bidding model makes sense here

Growth Boost works because Skool is spending its own ad budget on behalf of owners, which means that budget is a shared, finite resource across every community trying to use the feature at once. A flat allocation for everyone would either overspend on communities with, small or already-saturated audiences or underspend on ones ready to convert a lot more traffic profitably. A bidding system lets the market sort that out: owners who believe they can convert more visitors into paying members, and are willing to signal that with a higher bid, get more of the available reach. It's the same logic behind ad auctions on any platform, applied to a feature that started out as a flat, seemingly free boost.

## What to watch as an owner

If you're already running Growth Boost, the practical question this update raises is how to set the bid percentage without either overpaying for reach you don't need or underbidding and getting starved out by more aggressive communities in your niche. Since the feature is new, there isn't yet an established playbook for what a "good" bid looks like for a given niche or community size, that's likely to become clearer as more owners share results in the coming months.

## Why this matters if you're evaluating Skool communities

A bidding system for growth spend is one more reason to treat raw membership growth carefully when comparing communities. Two groups growing at the same rate could be doing it very differently now: one converting organic interest and word of mouth, the other simply outbidding competitors for platform ad reach. Neither is illegitimate, but they're not the same signal, and it's worth keeping in mind when growth velocity alone gets used as a stand-in for community quality.
