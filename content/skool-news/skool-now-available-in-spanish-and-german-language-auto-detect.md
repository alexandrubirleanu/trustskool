# Skool Is Now Available in Spanish and German: How the New Language Setting Works

Skool News #68 announces that the entire Skool interface, on desktop and mobile, is now available in Spanish and German, with automatic language detection on a visitor's first visit. It also rounds up Growth Boost wins and niche Skool of the Week picks.

## The whole Skool UI, translated

This is not a partial translation of a few settings pages. According to the hosts, everything in the Skool interface is translated, including the about page, the signup form and the credit card input form. Users can switch language at any time by clicking their profile and choosing a language.

## Auto-detection and saved preferences

On a person's first visit, Skool detects their language from their browser settings and renders the page in that language. If someone then picks a different language manually, Skool saves that preference. So a user whose browser is set to Spanish but who prefers English will keep seeing English when they return.

The practical benefit is for creators promoting to Spanish or German speaking audiences. When those visitors click a link and land on an about page, it shows up immediately in their native language, all the way through checkout. The hosts expect this to remove friction for non-English speakers and improve conversion rates.

## What is not translated yet

The one exception is email. Skool's email templates still go out in English, although the content inside them, such as posts written in Spanish, stays in whatever language the creator wrote it in. The team plans to get to emails eventually.

## More languages coming

Before expanding, the team wants to confirm Spanish and German work well, catching things like text wrapping onto two lines or buttons looking odd. French and Chinese are next. The hosts said most of the underlying work is now done, so adding more languages should not be too difficult. Anyone who spots a translation issue can report it in the Skoolers community or through support.

## Growth Boost keeps delivering

A large part of the episode was a run of Growth Boost success posts from community owners. Some of the numbers shared:

- A creator with over 200,000 followers said Growth Boost was outperforming his own audience, with the Skool network driving around 70% of his traffic.
- One owner went from 150 members built on her own content to 230 and climbing, with new members saying they had finally found their people.
- Another had 156 new members in 30 days, 95 of them from Skool.
- A brand new nutrition community got 17 signups in its first week, with Skool driving about half of its traffic.
- One owner reported 600 paying members in 37 days, and another said 92% of his traffic came from Skool.

Several owners said Skool's ads beat their own, and at least one cancelled his ads. The hosts said Growth Boost volume is still being turned up gradually.

## Skool of the Week: every niche works

Featured groups included a dance academy making $10,000 a month at $15 a month, a silver clay jewelry community at $9 a month with 243 members, a mural painting community at $49 a month making almost $3,000 a month, and a British gun making academy at $49 a month making $3,600 a month.

The takeaway was that the niche itself does not matter as much as having one, because Growth Boost cannot promote a group if it is unclear who it is for. One tip: search your niche on Google and look at the images that come up, then show that simplest visual version of your niche in your cover image and about page.

## Why it matters

For Skool communities serving Spanish and German speakers, localized signup and checkout is a meaningful conversion lever. For anyone evaluating communities, it also signals that Skool is pushing beyond English-speaking markets, which widens the pool of groups worth tracking.
