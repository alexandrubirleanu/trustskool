# Skool's Free Trial, Freemium Upgrade Button, and Close Door: Three Pricing Features Explained

Episode #66 of Skool News is a tight, feature-focused update covering three changes to how community owners handle trials, freemium plans, and enrollment: locked content during a free trial, a dedicated freemium upgrade button, and a new "close door" control.

## Free trial locked content

Skool already lets community owners offer a free trial period, commonly seven days, before a member's card is charged. The update covered in this episode adds locked content to that flow: owners can now designate certain posts, modules, or classroom content as inaccessible until the trial converts into a paid membership, rather than opening the entire group to trialing members by default. For owners running higher-ticket communities, that matters because a free trial with full access can let someone extract the core value (a course, a template pack, a swipe file) and cancel before the trial period ends. Gating specific content behind conversion closes that loophole without removing the trial itself, which keeps the low-friction signup Skool's growth features are built around.

## A dedicated freemium upgrade button

Skool's freemium model, a free tier and a paid tier living inside the same group, has been a recurring theme across recent episodes, including the two freemium creators (a $30k/month AI community and a $19,345/month Gut Health community) featured just a few episodes earlier. This update adds a direct upgrade button inside the freemium experience, giving free members an explicit, one-click path to the paid tier instead of relying on owners to manually prompt upgrades through posts or DMs. For a monetization model that depends entirely on converting free members into paying ones, removing friction from that single moment, the decision to upgrade, is one of the more direct levers Skool can pull on behalf of every freemium group at once.

## The "close door" control

The third change is a "close door" option, giving owners a way to stop new signups into a group (or a specific tier) without unpublishing the community or wiping its Discovery presence. This is a small but practical control for owners who are at capacity, mid-cohort, or intentionally running a waitlist model, situations where staying visible on Discovery is still valuable even while enrollment is temporarily paused.

## Why it matters if you're evaluating a Skool community

None of these three changes are algorithm updates, and Skool News #66 doesn't frame them as such. They're closer to plumbing: reducing the specific points of friction and leakage in Skool's trial-to-paid and free-to-paid pipelines. For anyone evaluating a Skool community from the outside, that matters indirectly. A platform that keeps tightening its own trial and freemium mechanics is one where the gap between a group's stated pricing and what its members actually experience getting there should keep narrowing, which is one more reason growth and retention data, not just sticker price, are what actually tell you whether a community is worth joining.
