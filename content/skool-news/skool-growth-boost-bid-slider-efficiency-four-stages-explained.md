# Skool Growth Boost Bidding Explained: The New Slider, Efficiency and the Four Boost Stages

Skool News #71 is a full episode on Growth Boost. It introduces a bid slider, explains how Skool measures efficiency, covers why a boost stalls and lays out the stages a group moves through.

## The new bid slider

In Settings, under the Discovery tab, groups with Growth Boost on can now change their bid. The default is 30%, meaning Skool takes 30% of revenue from members it brings in and the owner keeps 70%. Owners can raise that to 40% or 50%, or go to 60% or 70% for maximum members, trading profit for growth.

Key details:

- The change only applies to new members. Existing customers stay at the percentage they joined at.
- If Growth Boost already works for you, you may not need to touch it.
- If nothing is happening, the suggestion is to start high and lower it gradually once it works.
- It can take days or weeks for the system to fully respond, so changing it constantly is not a good idea.

## What efficiency means

The slider is not a normal auction where the highest bidder wins. The most efficient group gets the most traffic, and the bid percentage is just one part of efficiency. Skool defines efficiency as a group's ability to convert visitors into customers and keep them. It is based on:

- **Effective conversion rate:** about page conversion combined with free trial conversion. A 2% about page rate with a 50% trial conversion rate is a 1% effective rate.
- **ARPU:** average revenue per user rather than headline price, since tiers change what people actually pay.
- **Retention:** paid user retention over time.
- **Growth Boost percent:** the share Skool receives.

From those, Skool estimates how long it takes to earn back what it spends promoting a group. The shorter that payback period, the more traffic it sends. Skool says it already spends far more on Growth Boost than it earns from it. Going from 30% to 60% doubles a group's numbers instantly, but a weak group at 70% still gets little.

## Why a boost might not be working

The FAQ covered a few likely causes when stats look good but no ads appear, or a boost stops:

- The stats are not good enough yet, meaning payback would take too long.
- The system has not found and promoted the group yet, or other groups have better stats and take priority.
- Ads fatigued and the system did not replace them fast enough, which the hosts acknowledged is a current problem.

The fixes: drive more traffic, link your socials, improve your numbers, or raise the boost percent.

## The four Growth Boost stages

1. **Not enough data:** a group needs at least 100 about page visitors and one paid signup in the last 30 days from a single traffic source. These groups still appear in shared carousel ads.
2. **Stats not good enough:** enough data, but price, conversion, churn or boost percent do not work yet. Carousel ads continue at low spend to test for improvement.
3. **Good stats:** a dedicated campaign with its own budget and 20 or more custom creatives built from the about page, often $500 to $1,000 or more a day.
4. **Great stats:** a partnership campaign with more creatives, clipped social media content and $5,000 to $10,000 a day in spend.

## Other updates

Skool added Turkish, Japanese and Vietnamese, for about 11 languages in total, and will pause on new ones for now. Reviews passed 20,000, beat no reviews in a 50/50 test for groups with three or more, and were expected to go live for everyone that week once moderation and user reporting were in place.

## Why it matters

For owners, the slider is a quick lever while you work on the slower job of improving conversion and retention. For anyone evaluating communities, heavy Growth Boost traffic tends to go to groups that convert and retain well.
