# Skool's Free Trial and Freemium Update: Locked Content, a Faster Upgrade Button, and a 'Close the Door' Option

Skool News #66 is a shorter, feature-focused episode built around three related changes to how free trials and freemium communities work on Skool: the ability to lock specific content behind a paid tier during a free trial, a clearer freemium upgrade button, and a new "close the door" control for owners who want to stop offering free access after a point.

## Locking content during a free trial

Free trials on Skool have historically meant full access, a new member gets everything a paid member gets, for a limited window, then either converts or loses access entirely. The update covered in this episode lets owners lock specific content, a module, a category, a set of posts, behind the paid tier even while a member is inside their trial period. In practice that turns the trial from "try everything for free" into "try enough to see the value, then hit a wall that shows you what you're missing." For community owners running trials as a conversion tool rather than a goodwill gesture, that distinction matters: a trial that gives away the entire offer has less reason to convert than one that deliberately holds something back.

## A dedicated freemium upgrade button

The second piece is a clearer, more direct upgrade button for freemium communities, the model where a community has a permanent free tier alongside a paid one, rather than a time-limited trial. Freemium only works as a growth engine if free members can find the upgrade path without hunting for it, and a purpose-built button in the member experience is a straightforward fix for a common freemium complaint: people who'd pay if the option were obvious, but don't go looking for it. It's a small UI change, but it sits directly on top of the funnel every freemium community depends on, free signups need a visible, low-friction next step, or the free tier just becomes free forever.

## The "close the door" option

The third change gives owners a way to stop new members from joining for free once they decide a freemium or trial phase has run its course, effectively closing the free door while keeping existing free members in place, or converting acquisition entirely to paid. This is aimed at owners who used a free or trial phase deliberately to seed a community with members and content, and now want to shift the growth model without disrupting people who already joined under the old terms.

## Why it matters

None of these three changes are algorithm updates or ranking changes, they're monetization tooling. But they matter for anyone evaluating a Skool community for the same reason TrustSkool tracks pricing stability as part of its scoring: a community that can lock content mid-trial, add a real upgrade path, or shut off free access are all decisions that change what a "free" listing on Skool actually means to a prospective member, and how a community's stated price can drift with little warning to people evaluating it from outside.
