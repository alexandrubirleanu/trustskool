# Skool Goes Global: Fast Payouts in 7 New Countries, Reviews A/B Test Results and New Languages

Skool News #70 covers three internationalization updates: fast payouts in India, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Peru and Costa Rica, early results from A/B testing community reviews, and new interface languages. It closes with a roadmap check, Growth Boost wins and Skool of the Week.

## Global fast payouts

Skool now supports payouts in India, Brazil, Mexico, Argentina, Chile, Peru and Costa Rica. Before this, creators in India could not get paid out at all, and in some of the other countries payouts were either unavailable or slow, sometimes taking around 10 days. Now payouts are fully supported and fast, with some creators in India receiving funds in under a minute.

These seven countries are just the start. The plan is to roll fast payouts out to every country except sanctioned ones Skool cannot legally pay, with the hosts expecting it to be done within the month.

## Reviews: early numbers and A/B test results

Reviews launched the week before and were initially visible only to members and admins while Skool collected reviews and tested their effect. In under a week, almost 15,000 reviews were collected from verified paying customers, with nearly 3,800 on launch day and about 1,200 the day before recording. The average rating shown was 4.93, which the hosts said looks almost too high.

Skool then A/B tested reviews versus no reviews on groups with five or more reviews. Showing reviews performed better. The more surprising finding was that groups with a few negative reviews converted better than groups with a perfect 5.0. The hosts' explanation is that a visible bad review makes the good ones more believable, while a flawless score can look too good to be true.

Skool is also adding an internal system that flags incoming reviews, including every negative one, for a team to check. The goal is to remove entries that are not real reviews, such as a one-star post that just asks how to cancel. This process is invisible to owners and members.

On who can review, the answer is only actively paying members. Free members and people who paid off-platform cannot leave reviews, because opening that up would invite abuse and make reviews untrustworthy.

Reviews will go public gradually. Groups with a few reviews (around three was floated) may get them public first, while groups with none stay hidden longer to build them up. Platform-wide rollout is expected by the end of the month.

## New languages: Chinese, Italian and Dutch

Skool is now available in English, Spanish, German, French, Portuguese, Chinese, Italian and Dutch, with more to come. Growth Boost has also started running ads in Spanish. One owner saw new members jump from five to 25 a day, which the hosts attributed to Spanish ads leading to fully Spanish about pages. Local currencies and payment methods are planned next.

## Roadmap

Reviews, languages and global payouts should all be complete by the end of the month. The efficiency score is getting close, Growth Boost optimization continues, and payment methods and currencies are up next. The stated focus is growth tools and internationalization.

## Growth Boost wins and Skool of the Week

Wins shared included a creator with 525,000 Instagram followers who said Skool is now his top growth channel, an owner who went from $84 to $1,058 in MRR, and a creator who took a year to reach $3,000 MRR on Substack but hit $4,000 a month within 30 days on Skool at $12 a month. Featured communities included Oscar Patel's health and looks community at $275,000 a month, a screenwriting group run by a Shrek 2 screenwriter at $10,000 a month, and a work-from-home job leads group at $60,000 a month.

## Why it matters

Verified, paying-member-only reviews give prospective members a new trust signal, and Skool's own data suggests an imperfect score can help conversion. Faster payouts and more languages mean more strong communities will come from outside English-speaking markets.
