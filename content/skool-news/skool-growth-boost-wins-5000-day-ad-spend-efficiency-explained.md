# Skool Growth Boost Is Working: $5,000 a Day Ad Spend and What Efficient Really Means

Skool News #67 reports that Growth Boost has improved sharply in a single week, with efficient groups now getting $5,000 or more a day in dedicated ad spend. The hosts share owner wins, a checklist for making Growth Boost work, and a breakdown of what "efficient" means in the upcoming efficiency score.

## Growth Boost wins across very different niches

The episode opens with a run of success stories, and the niches are deliberately varied:

- A fantasy crochet community at $5 a month where the Skool network was providing about 98% of new members after the owner went back to work.
- A guitar teacher whose daily visitors rose from around 20 to 240.
- A plumbing business community run by a father and son that received 18,000 visitors from Skool. They had been spending $20,000 a month on their own ads, stopped entirely, and said profit rose over 50%.
- A group that got 321 members in seven days after its owner switched it from free to paid and updated the about page, at one point reaching number three in trending.

The hosts stressed that Growth Boost helps both new creators and those with audiences of 100,000 or more.

## Dedicated campaigns and repurposed content

Once a group proves efficient, it gets its own campaigns, custom creatives and dedicated spend. Efficient groups are getting $5,000 a day or more, and the hosts expect some to reach $10,000 a day, or about $300,000 a month. Growth Boost is also pulling in owners' organic social videos and running them as ads. The hosts said they feel they have barely started.

## How to make Growth Boost work for you

The checklist (about half an hour of work):

1. **Improve your packaging.** Your cover image, description and about page are what people see in Discovery and in ads. Make it immediately clear what the group is and who it is for.
2. **Link your social profiles.** Go to Settings, Profile, Social links and add YouTube, X, Instagram and others. Growth Boost uses these to understand your audience and to find content it can repurpose into ads.
3. **Put your Skool link directly in your bio** on every platform. Link aggregators like Linktree or Stan Store do not count, and direct links will get a bias in the algorithm.
4. **Add your link to YouTube descriptions,** including bulk editing your back catalog.
5. **Drive your own traffic to the about page** to create history and data. The groups getting the most boost also tend to drive their own traffic.

## What "efficient" means

Skool plans to show a simple efficiency dashboard. Efficiency is Skool's ability to take someone from the internet, get their attention, convert them into a paying member and keep them. The components:

- **A clear market niche.** Niche matters more than topic. A niche within a niche, like fantasy crochet, can make an ad feel made for the viewer.
- **Price.** Groups from about $5 to $200 a month are working, with most between roughly $20 and $100.
- **About page conversion rate.** Above 1% is good, and many top groups sit around 1%. Rates naturally fall as traffic grows.
- **Trial conversion rate,** if you use trials. 30% to 40% or more is good.
- **Retention.** Above 80% is a good target, though Skool looks mostly at cohorted MRR retention by month.

The score currently has a lag, so groups with a few months of history have an advantage.

## Skool of the Week

Featured were a microscopy exam prep group at $97 a month making $13,500 a month and an electronics repair group at $34 a month making $5,600 a month, with Skool driving 54% of its traffic.

## Why it matters

The efficiency components are a useful lens for evaluating any community: clear niche, sensible price, real conversion and retention. Groups strong on all of them are the ones Skool is now putting serious ad money behind.
