# Skool's Plan to End Creator Burnout: Growth Boost Vision and the Big Projects Ahead

With a batch of features recently shipped and bigger projects underway, Skool News #75 steps back to explain the problem Skool is trying to solve, how Growth Boost fits in, and what is coming next.

## The mission: 1 billion people in community

Skool's stated vision is to help 1 billion people find community, and to get there by helping 1 million people go full-time doing what they love. The internet, the hosts argue, makes a living possible in almost any niche, even donkey hoof trimming.

## The problem: the content treadmill

The catch is creator burnout. Creators are pushed to keep producing more content, for more platforms, in more formats: blogs and newsletters, then long-form YouTube, then Shorts, TikTok, vertical video, community posts and lives, plus ads, creatives and tracking. They also argue that because platform algorithms optimize for attention to sell ads, the loudest creators tend to win rather than the best ones.

## The idea: a good community that grows itself

Today, the hosts estimate, about 80% of a creator's time goes to content and 20% to their craft and community. Skool wants to flip that. In a poll in the Skoolers community, they say about 95% of creators preferred the flipped version, including creators with 500,000 or more subscribers. The aim is that you bring your niche and show up for members, and Skool provides growth. Making your own content still helps, it just shouldn't be the only path.

They say this already works for some groups. They have seen creators post that Growth Boost brings more signups than a 300,000-subscriber YouTube channel, and one case where it delivered 10 times the signups of a 400,000-person audience. About two and a half months after Growth Boost launched, one host said he is now almost certain the model can work, and that the team is getting close.

## Coming soon: partnership video ads and more visibility

Growth Boost remains the main focus. A new feature lets qualifying groups run co-branded campaigns with Skool using their own video ads. Once a group reaches a certain efficiency score, it can connect its Instagram and Facebook accounts, upload about five videos (such as its best organic posts), see how each performs and replenish them. Skool tested this manually before building it.

Skool also plans to show owners whether ads are running for them and which ones. It does not plan to show raw efficiency scores yet, because the score varies by traffic source and can drop for reasons that aren't the group's fault, such as a burst of off-niche traffic. Showing which stage a group is in is the preferred approach.

## The rest of the roadmap

- **Payment methods:** local options like PayPal in Germany, Pix in Brazil, and WeChat Pay and Alipay in parts of Asia, with checkout adapting to language, currency and payment method.
- **Currencies:** viewing and paying in your own currency.
- **Native app:** making the app as fast and fluid as Instagram or WhatsApp, especially far from the US.
- **Live calls:** more people now use Skool Live than Meet or Zoom, the hosts say. The goal is Zoom-level stability on weak connections.
- **Global calendar and search:** one view of what's live now and upcoming across all your groups, plus an easier way to find people and groups.

## Skool of the week

## Why it matters

If Skool's network can supply growth to good groups, a community's results will depend more on retention and member experience than on its owner's posting volume. That makes real growth data a more useful signal than audience size.
