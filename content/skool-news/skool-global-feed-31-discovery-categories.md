# Skool Global Feed: See Posts From All Your Groups in One Place, Plus 31 Discovery Categories

Skool News #81 reveals that the "secret project" on the roadmap was Global Feed, a single feed showing posts from every group you belong to. The episode also expands Discovery from 9 categories to 31, which flows through to the leaderboards.

## What Global Feed does

Global Feed is reached through the group switcher. It shows the best recent posts from all the groups you are in, in one place. Across the top it lists the communities you have used most recently, which updates automatically, and flags any that are live right now. Clicking a group at the top, or the group name inside a post, takes you straight into that community.

It is opt-in by design. Nothing changes for members who never open it: they still land on their default group feed or the last group they visited. It is aimed at people who belong to several groups.

## The problem it solves

The hosts describe a familiar pattern. Someone joins around four groups, spends nearly all their time in one, and forgets the others exist, or finds it too much effort to switch over and scroll each feed. In internal testing, people saw posts from groups they had forgotten about, liked them and engaged more. The interest was there; they had just forgotten.

The hosts expect several knock-on effects:

- **More live attendance**, since you can see at a glance when a group is live and drop in.
- **More engagement** for most groups, because posts surface to members who would not have visited.
- **More joins.** One host found his feed was all business groups and joined health and fitness groups to round it out. Testers also said they were finally willing to join paid groups they had held off on, because they knew they would actually see them.
- **Better stickiness**, which the hosts tie directly to retention and engagement.

## Discovery goes from 9 to 31 categories

Some of the old categories were enormous. Money in particular lumped together very different kinds of groups, as did hobbies. Skool analyzed where there is real demand, such as Discovery searches and purchases from ads, and where there is supply, meaning groups that exist, and settled on 31 categories. Money is now split into areas like AI automation, AI video, side hustles, ads and marketing, e-commerce, trading, real estate, credit and sales. The leaderboards use the same categories, so you can see the top groups in a narrower niche.

## Roadmap update

- **Global Feed** has shipped.
- **Live Calls V2**, rebuilt to match Zoom on stability and performance, is being tested live that week, with a wider release planned for the following week if it goes well. The hosts expect live calls to become more central, especially now that Global Feed shows what is live.
- **Payment methods** are in progress, with a global checkout that adapts to where the buyer is, mentioning options like PayPal and WeChat.
- **Global search** is up next, so you can find a user, group or post from one search box. **Currencies** and a **native app** follow.

## Why this matters for people evaluating Skool communities

Global Feed lowers the cost of belonging to more than one group, which could help smaller or secondary communities keep members who would otherwise drift away. The finer Discovery and leaderboard categories also make it easier to compare a group against real peers in its niche, rather than against everything filed under money.
