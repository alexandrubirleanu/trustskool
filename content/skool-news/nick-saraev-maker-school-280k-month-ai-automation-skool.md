# Nick Saraev's Maker School: How an AI Automation Skool Hit $280,000/Month With No Team

Nick Saraev runs Maker School, a Skool community that teaches people to land their first client for an AI or automation service. In this Platinum call he walks through how he set it up, priced it, launched it and runs it alone, at what the hosts describe as roughly 95% profit margins.

## Launch: test in the market, not your mind

Saraev's starting point was deliberately minimal. Day one of Maker School was a simple 14-day agency program and one monthly call on the calendar, with no polished video. At the time he had a following he estimates at around 10,000. He DMed people on Twitter who had engaged with his posts, got 20 to 30 beta testers inside, and asked them what they thought. Most said it was weak, so he rebuilt it from their feedback.

The public launch was a soft one. Late at night he added a link to the community at the top of his YouTube descriptions, went to bed, and woke up to a few hundred dollars in MRR.

## Pricing: start low, raise on member milestones

He believes the launch price was about $46 or $47 a month, picked after glancing at competitors charging anywhere from $20 to $100. Raising a price, he reasoned, is easier than lowering it. Maker School now sits at $184 a month. Rather than time-based deadlines, he ties price increases to member counts, which he says creates a squeeze as the next milestone approaches.

He was candid about one regret: he raised prices too quickly and too high. Looking back, he would have preferred a lower price, more members and a stronger network effect, and he plans to hold at $184 for now.

## The offer: a yes-or-no guarantee

The piece of the business he credits most is the promise: land your first AI client in 90 days, or get your money back. Coming from door-to-door sales and cold email, he wanted an outcome a buyer either achieves or doesn't, with as little risk as possible. He estimates refund requests run around 1 to 3% of revenue, while he puts the upside of having the guarantee at roughly 300%, which the video description frames as a 300% increase in signups. The hosts called this a boolean offer: binary, either achieved or not.

## About page, content and promotion

- His original about page video was a plain, unscripted talk, and he says it converted well. The image gallery is packed with member wins.
- He uploads the about page video through Loom because, at the time, it autoplayed frames, and he saw a lift in conversion.
- He pitches at roughly a 99% value to 1% sales ratio, often mentioning Maker School once at the end of long tutorials, and relies on links in descriptions instead of repeated verbal CTAs.
- YouTube drives about 20 times more conversions than Instagram for him, despite similar follower counts, roughly matching a 20x gap in watch time.
- About 28.1% of his traffic comes from the Skool network. Adding "AI Automation" to his group name moved him from page five or six to page one of Discovery search for that term.

## Running the community

Every morning he replies to most posts from the previous 24 hours, using a voice transcription tool to answer faster, and makes one small improvement to the community each day. The classroom is split into a resource library, short courses and a day-by-day main program. Recording short videos for common questions cut his daily management time from 70 to 80 minutes to 40 to 50. He also recently added an annual plan at a 30% discount.

## Why it matters for Skool communities

Maker School shows that a solo operator can scale on Skool with a simple funnel: content, a link in the bio, and an about page with a concrete promise. For anyone evaluating communities in this niche, consistent daily owner presence, a clear guarantee and steady rather than spiky growth are the signals worth looking for.
