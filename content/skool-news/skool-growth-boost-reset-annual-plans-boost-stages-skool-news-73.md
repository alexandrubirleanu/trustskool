# Skool Growth Boost Reset: Annual Plans, Free Trial Checks and the Four Boost Stages

Skool News #73 opens with two small features, reviews in Google search and automatic language detection, then spends most of its time on Growth Boost: why it was reset, what group owners can do to qualify, and how the system decides how much to spend on a community.

## Reviews in Google and auto language detection

Skool reviews now appear in Google results when someone searches for a group by name, in some cases above the creator's YouTube channel. Skool also now detects a visitor's language and translates both the interface and the content across Discovery and about pages. The hosts showed Chinese, Spanish and German versions, which effectively means a separate Discovery per language. Suggested communities, ads, currencies and local payment methods are planned to follow.

## The Growth Boost reset

Skool stopped its Growth Boost campaigns and rebuilt them from scratch across different ad accounts to apply what it learned from the first version. Performance is already better, but the team expects one to two weeks to get back to previous spend levels. If a group's boost dipped recently, the hosts say that may be the reset rather than something the owner did wrong.

## Five tips to make Growth Boost work for you

1. **Put your Skool link in your bio everywhere**, plus YouTube descriptions, so Skool can see which traffic source converts.
2. **Drive traffic to your about page** with reels, stories and mentions in videos.
3. **Improve your packaging.** That means the cover image, title, description and about page. The cover image also runs as your ad, so a stranger should understand the group at a glance.
4. **Add an annual option** with a 20 to 50% discount. Under 20% does little, and the groups doing well tend to sit at 30 to 50%. Annual cash up front lets Growth Boost recoup spend faster. Examples shown included 30%, 49% and 67% discounts.
5. **Start the boost slider high, then dial it back.** Moving from the default 30 to 70 can more than double a group's efficiency score, and the hosts saw groups jump into the efficient tier with one change.

They also flagged free trials. The platform average trial conversion is around 40%, but individual groups range from about 8% to 80%. Growth Boost now calculates trial conversion per group, so a poorly converting trial can keep a group from being promoted.

## How the four boost stages work

Skool scores groups on conversion rate, price, retention and boost percentage to estimate how quickly it can earn back ad spend. The stages are:

- **Not enough data:** you need at least 100 about page visitors and one paid signup in the last 30 days from a specific traffic source. Groups here get a trickle via carousel ads.
- **Enough data, stats not good enough:** still a carousel ad using your cover image, used to retest your stats.
- **Good stats:** a dedicated campaign with 20+ creatives, roughly $1,000 to $5,000 a day in spend.
- **Great stats:** a partnership campaign at $5,000 to $10,000+ a day, with clips of your own social content.

Low-priced groups, around $10 or less, need especially strong stats. The hosts also noted that Discovery's trending mix had skewed toward $9 groups and is being rebalanced, and urged owners not to reprice their business based on what is trending this week.

## Skool of the week

F The Gurus, a sales community run by a former special forces sniper, is at about $95,000 MRR with 52% of traffic from the Skool network, and serves as the example of a stage-four partnership campaign. An interior design group is at about $8,300 a month, and a car design community at $59 a month makes about $4,000.

## Why it matters

Growth Boost rewards the same things members care about: clear packaging, conversion and retention. For people evaluating communities, groups that qualify for heavy boost spend are, by Skool's own math, ones that keep paying members.
