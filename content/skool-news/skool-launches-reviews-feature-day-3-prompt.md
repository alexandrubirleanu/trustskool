# Skool Launches a Native Reviews Feature (Skool News #69)

Episode #69 announces a feature Skool community owners have been asking for since the platform started scaling into the hundreds of thousands of groups: native reviews. Rather than owners relying on screenshots of DMs or member testimonials pasted into a sales page, ratings and feedback are now something the platform itself collects and surfaces.

## What's shipping

The mechanic described in the episode is a timed prompt: new members get asked to leave a review on day 3 of joining a community, early enough that the experience is fresh, but late enough that they've had a real first impression of the group rather than just the signup flow. That timing choice matters. A review requested on day one would mostly capture onboarding friction; a review requested a week or two in risks a much lower response rate as initial enthusiasm fades. Day 3 is Skool's bet on the sweet spot between signal quality and response volume.

## Why this is a bigger deal than it looks

Up to this point, the only ways to judge whether a Skool community was actually worth joining were the About Page pitch, member count, activity level, and word of mouth, none of which are hard to inflate or fake. A built-in review system, if it holds up over time, gives prospective members and outside observers a data point that lives inside the platform rather than being curated by the owner. It also puts real pressure on communities that oversell in their marketing but underdeliver once someone's inside: a wave of lukewarm day-3 reviews is a much harder thing to hide than a slow trickle of unsubscribes.

It's worth watching how Skool handles the obvious risks that come with any review system: whether reviews are gated to verified members only, whether owners get any ability to respond, and how the platform prevents an active minority (very happy fans, or people with a grudge) from skewing early average scores on smaller communities where a handful of reviews can swing the number heavily.

## Why this matters if you're evaluating Skool communities

For anyone trying to decide which Skool community is worth their time or money, native reviews are the single most useful signal Skool has added to its own discovery experience so far, more direct than growth metrics or trending status, because it's built from actual member sentiment rather than acquisition activity. It also raises the bar for third-party trackers and directories: a review layer inside Skool itself means external growth and trust scoring now has another real data point to weigh against member count and pricing stability.
