# Growth Boost Is Boosting: Skool Says Efficient Groups Are Now Seeing Real Results

Episode #67 of Skool News picks up the Growth Boost story from where episode #62 left off. Back then, the hosts were candid that results were inconsistent, "raining in some places and not others," and that real optimization was still weeks out. This episode's headline, "Growth boost is starting to rip," is Skool's update on where that experiment has landed after further tuning.

## Success stories are starting to stack up

The episode opens by framing Growth Boost as now producing a meaningful run of success stories rather than the scattered, hit-or-miss results reported a month earlier. Among the examples cited, some newer creators are seeing 30% or more of their members arrive directly through Skool's own Discovery and ad distribution, without those creators driving that traffic themselves. Notably, the examples aren't limited to the AI, marketing, or make-money niches that tend to dominate Discovery's trending tab. Smaller, hobby-focused communities, ukulele and machine knitting were both mentioned, are picking up members before the owner has done any external promotion at all. That's a meaningful signal: it suggests Growth Boost's distribution isn't just rewarding already-large or already-trending categories, but is finding audience demand in niches that would otherwise have no organic discovery path on the platform.

## "Efficient groups" get more of the boost

The second half of the episode returns to a concept first previewed back in episode #62: an efficiency rating that determines how much of Growth Boost's traffic a given group receives. The framing here is consistent with what Skool said earlier, groups with strong "packaging" (clear about pages, focused descriptions, a well-set cover image) and healthy on-platform conversion get sent disproportionately more traffic, while weaker-packaged groups see less benefit even if they're otherwise a good fit for the algorithm. Skool's message in this episode is that the feature has moved from an unpredictable early rollout into something owners can actually influence by fixing their own packaging, rather than something that only happens to them.

## Why it matters if you're evaluating a Skool community

Growth Boost adds a wrinkle to reading raw member counts: a community's size increasingly reflects not just its own marketing but how efficiently Skool's own algorithm is currently routing traffic to it, a variable outside the owner's direct control and one that's still being tuned by Skool itself. A community that looks like it's growing fast this month could be riding a platform-level tailwind rather than genuine demand or retention. That's exactly the kind of signal that a simple member count can't separate out, and why growth trends over time, not a single snapshot, are the more honest way to judge whether a Skool community is actually building something durable.
