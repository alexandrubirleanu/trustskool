# Skool's First $1,000,000/Month Community: Logan Sendle's Blue-Collar Biz Accelerator (Skool News #72)

Episode #72 marks a milestone the channel has been building toward all year: a Skool community crossing $1,000,000 in monthly recurring revenue for the first time. Earlier episodes tracked "the race to $1M/month" as a running storyline; this episode names the winner.

## Who got there first

The record goes to Logan Sendle, whose community, Blue-Collar Biz Accelerator, serves home-service and trades business owners, landscapers, hardscapers, lawn care operators, excavation contractors, general contractors, painters, power-washers, window cleaners, and similar trades, who want more leads, better hires, and a business built to scale past where most owner-operators plateau. The program is aimed specifically at businesses doing roughly $50,000 to $500,000 a year in revenue, helping them accelerate toward seven figures.

Sendle's own background matches the niche closely: he owned and operated a landscaping and hardscaping company in Minnesota for seven years before selling it in May 2024. That kind of direct operator experience, rather than a marketing or coaching background applied to an unfamiliar industry, is a recurring pattern among the highest-earning Skool communities this channel has profiled: the founder actually did the work before teaching it. Reported results from the accelerator back up the traction, more than 120 contractors taken from their first dollar of revenue to a combined $4 million in just seven months.

## Why blue-collar, and why now

Trades businesses are a niche that's easy to underestimate from inside the online business and course-creator world Skool started in, but it's a large, underserved market: skilled labor is in chronically short supply, margins in trades work can be strong once a business scales past a one-truck operation, and most trades owners have had nowhere structured to learn business operations, hiring, and growth systems built for their specific industry. A $1M/month outcome in this niche is a signal to anyone building a Skool community that vertical-specific, operator-led programs outside the usual coaching-and-marketing crowd can outperform more crowded, generalist niches.

## Why this matters if you're evaluating Skool communities

This episode is as much a data point about niche selection as it is about one founder's success. The communities setting revenue records on Skool increasingly aren't the ones chasing the biggest, most obvious audiences, they're narrow, credibility-backed programs serving an industry the founder actually worked in. If you're sizing up Skool communities to join or benchmarking against competitors, Blue-Collar Biz Accelerator is worth studying less for the trades-specific content and more for the structural pattern: real operator credibility, a tightly defined buyer, and pricing built for a market that can support high-ticket fees because the ROI (leads, hires, revenue growth) is concrete and measurable.
