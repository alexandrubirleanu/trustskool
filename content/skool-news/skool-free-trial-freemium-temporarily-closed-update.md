# Skool Adds Free Trial Locking and a 'Temporarily Closed' Status (Skool News #66)

Episode #66 of Skool News, recorded live from Skool HQ with Sam Ovens and Andrew Kirby, is a straight product update focused on two related parts of the platform: how free trials convert into paying members, and what happens to a community when an owner isn't actively running it as a paid product.

## Locked content during the free trial

The headline change tightens up what a free-trial member can actually see. Historically, a 7-day free trial on a paid community risked giving visitors full access to everything, coursework, classroom content, past posts, with no real incentive to convert once the trial ended. The update lets owners lock specific content behind the paid tier even during a free trial, so a prospective member gets enough of a preview to see the value without consuming the entire product for free. Paired with that is a freemium upgrade button, a persistent, visible prompt nudging free or trialing members toward the paid plan rather than relying on them to find the upgrade flow on their own.

For any owner running a free-to-paid funnel, a group with a free tier feeding a premium tier, this is worth turning on directly. Locked content plus a visible upgrade path is a standard SaaS conversion pattern, and Skool bringing it natively into group settings removes a workaround owners were previously handling manually with post visibility rules or classroom permissions.

## Temporarily Closed: pausing without losing the group

The second feature addresses a different problem: owners who need to step away from a community, whether for a launch gap between cohorts, a personal break, or a pause on new signups, without shutting the group down entirely. The new "Temporarily Closed" status lets an owner pause access for members rather than deleting the community or leaving it in a payment-lapsed state. It's a small addition, but it fills a real gap: previously, an owner's only real levers were staying fully open or letting a subscription lapse, neither of which cleanly communicates "we're on pause, come back later" to existing members.

## Why this matters if you're tracking Skool communities

Neither change is a platform overhaul, but both point in the same direction Skool has been leaning all year: giving owners finer-grained control over the member lifecycle, from the first free-trial click to a pause in active operations, rather than treating every community as either fully open or fully shut. For anyone evaluating communities on Skool, tighter trial-to-paid conversion tooling is a signal that owners have more reason to invest in retention mechanics rather than just top-of-funnel growth. And a formal "paused" state means a quiet or inactive-looking community isn't automatically a red flag; it may simply be between cohorts rather than abandoned.

If you run a free trial funnel into a paid Skool group, this episode is worth five minutes to check whether locked content and the upgrade button are configured the way you want. If you've ever needed to step away from a group without killing it outright, Temporarily Closed is the setting to know about.
