# The 4 Levels of Skool Growth Boost: Requirements, Ad Spend and Benefits Explained

Skool News #78 gives the clearest look yet at how Growth Boost works, with a redesigned Growth tab that shows four levels, what each one unlocks and the exact 30-day requirements to reach it. The episode also changes how reviews are ordered on about pages.

## Reviews now sort by top

About pages used to show the newest review first, so a two-star review or a "how do I cancel" comment could land at the top. Reviews now sort by top by default, though visitors can still switch to most recent. The ranking favors higher star ratings, reviews with more written text, and reviewers who have been paying longer and are still paying members.

Skool also fixed the "still paying member after X months" label. Previously, someone who left and rejoined was only credited for their latest stint. It now adds up the total months they have actually paid.

## The four Growth Boost levels

The Growth tab shows your current level, the benefits at each level, the requirements, and your live ads with when they were last updated.

- **Level 1: cover image in carousel.** No custom ads yet, but your cover image is now displayed better inside Skool's carousel ads.
- **Level 2: 10 custom ads.** Requires 100 about page visitors and one new customer in the last 30 days.
- **Level 3: dedicated campaigns.** 30 custom ads and up to $1,000 a day in ad spend in your own campaign. Requires 3,000 about page visitors, a Skool link in bio on your socials, consistent organic promotion, and 10 Skool network sign-ups (paid) in the last 30 days. The hosts are considering letting level 3 groups upload their own static ads.
- **Level 4: partnership ads and your own video ads.** Up to $10,000 a day in dedicated spend, which the hosts point out is about $300,000 a month. Requires 10,000 about page visitors, link in bio, consistent organic promotion and 30 Skool network sign-ups in the last 30 days. Ads can run from your own handle in collaboration with Skool.

## Levels unlock spend, efficiency keeps it

Owners had been asking for the "efficiency score" Skool mentioned earlier. The hosts explain it is too volatile and noisy, and split by traffic source, to be a useful thing to show. The levels replace it as the visible target. Hitting a level does not guarantee daily spend, though: ads still have to work. If spending does not produce sign-ups, it will not continue. Gaming the requirements is basically pointless.

## How the ads are made and refreshed

Creatives are built from your cover image, about page text and price. Skool takes your existing look and puts it into ad formats it has seen work, rather than inventing a new style. A big change to your price, cover image or about page should trigger a refresh, generally within about an hour. Ad counts may start lower than 10 and will shift as Skool tests new angles across all groups at once.

## Early wins shared

Several owners posted results. One said he switched from Circle and now gets 71% of his traffic from the Skool network. Others reported 87% and 76% of customers or sign-ups coming from the Skool network, and one said Growth Boost beat his own $20,000 a month ad spend.

## Skool of the week

The featured groups were a chess improvement group at about $5,300 a month, a new mental math group at about $17,000 a month, an AI filmmaking group at about $35,000 a month, and a credit repair community at about $76,000 a month on a $37 price.

## Why this matters for people evaluating Skool communities

The level requirements turn Growth Boost into something measurable: about page traffic, consistent promotion and paid sign-ups. A community that sits at level 3 or 4 has shown real demand over the last 30 days, and top-sorted reviews now favor long-term paying members.
