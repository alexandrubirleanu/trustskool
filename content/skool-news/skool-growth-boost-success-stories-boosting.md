# Growth Boost Is Boosting: Skool's Paid-Ad Feature Starts Producing Real Numbers (Skool News #67)

Episode #67 is a progress check on Growth Boost, the feature Skool rolled out earlier this year where the platform itself spends money on ads to bring new members into a community, rather than requiring the owner to run their own campaigns. A few months after launch, the headline of this episode is simple: the feature is working, and owners are starting to talk about it publicly.

## What Growth Boost actually does

For anyone who hasn't turned it on, Growth Boost puts a community's About Page in front of new audiences through ads that Skool pays for and manages on the owner's behalf. The owner doesn't touch ad accounts, write copy, or manage a budget; the traffic and conversion mechanics sit inside Skool's own infrastructure, feeding directly into a community's membership funnel and, if enough signups follow, into Discovery placement and trending status.

## Early results owners are reporting

The kind of outcomes circulating this summer give a sense of what "boosting" looks like in practice. One owner, Lizzie Townsend, picked up 79 new paying customers in a single 7-day window after turning the feature on, with her community landing on Skool's Trending page and hitting a new all-time MRR record in the process. Another, Evan Shawn, saw Growth Boost drive more than 10,000 visits to his About Page in seven days, converting into 100-plus new paying members and a first-page spot in Discovery search results.

Numbers like that matter because they're the opposite of a vanity metric: traffic, conversion into paying members, and downstream visibility (Trending, Discovery) all moving together is the actual test of whether platform-funded ads are worth the tradeoff owners make by handing acquisition over to Skool.

## Why this matters if you're evaluating Skool communities

Growth Boost changes one of the harder things to judge from the outside: whether a community's member count is growing because of genuine demand or because an owner is quietly buying visibility. As more owners lean on it, membership growth and Discovery rank become less pure signals of organic demand and more a mix of content quality and how aggressively a community is using Skool's own ad spend. That's not a criticism of the feature, it's a legitimate growth lever, but it's a reason to weight growth velocity alongside other signals (retention, pricing stability, member activity) rather than treating fast member growth alone as proof of a healthy community.

For owners, the takeaway from this episode is straightforward: if Growth Boost is available on your plan and you haven't tried it, the reported results so far suggest it's worth a test window rather than dismissing it as another feature nobody uses. For everyone else sizing up which communities on Skool are actually worth joining, this is one more reason growth alone isn't the full picture.
