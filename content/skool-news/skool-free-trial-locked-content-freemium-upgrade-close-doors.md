# Skool Free Trial Locked Content, Freemium Upgrade Button and Close Doors Explained

Skool News #66 covers three monetization features: a "close doors" switch for pausing new signups, the ability to lock content and member DMs behind the end of a free trial, and a prominent upgrade button for free members in freemium groups. It also covers the roadmap and a lesson on focusing on traffic.

## Close doors: the right way to pause new members

Under Settings and then Pricing, owners now have a "close doors" option. It temporarily stops new members from joining or requesting membership, while existing members keep their access and keep being billed. The group is temporarily unlisted from Discovery, and the join button on the outside simply reads closed.

Previously, some owners were pausing signups by switching a paid group to free and ignoring membership requests, or by setting a fake price like $10,000. Both approaches hurt the group. Unapproved requests and about page visitors who never buy tell the Discovery algorithm the group is performing badly, so it gets pushed down in search and promotion. With close doors, stats and rank keep calculating in the background without penalty, and reopening returns the group to where it was.

## Free trial locked content

For paid groups with a free trial, owners can now mark content as off limits to trial members. This works at the course level and also per module or page inside a course, similar to how drip settings work. A trial member who opens locked content sees a prompt to end their trial to unlock it. One click confirms the upgrade, and because the card is already on file, they do not need to re-enter payment details.

Chat is gated too. Trial members can DM admins and admins can DM them, but trial members cannot DM other members. This is aimed at spammers who abused trials to message members and then cancel. The expectation is better trial conversion, less freeloading, and possibly room to shorten trials below seven days.

## A visible upgrade button for freemium groups

Building on the freemium update from the previous week, free members inside a freemium group now see a large yellow upgrade button on desktop, with settings moved just below it. On mobile it sits at the top. The button only appears when an upgrade option exists, and it links straight to the plans page showing the member's current tier. It solves a common freemium problem: free members not knowing a paid tier exists.

They also tied these changes to Growth Boost: a more efficient free trial or freemium funnel should improve a group's efficiency score, which means Growth Boost pushes it harder.

## Roadmap update

Work has started on an efficiency score so owners can see whether Growth Boost is working and what to fix; groups with history are easier to score than new ones. Some efficient groups are already getting around $5,000 a day in Growth Boost ad spend with dedicated campaigns. Global fast payouts are in final stages and languages were described as roughly two weeks out, followed by a secret project, payment methods and currencies.

## Max traffic by minimizing everything else

Following the previous week's "traffic maxing" idea, the hosts argued that top earners usually run one social platform plus one Skool community and cut most other tasks. They suggested logging your time for a week and sorting it into generating traffic or not. Conversion tweaks matter, but they said you will not 10x a business through conversion rate alone, while 10x traffic can.

## Skool of the week

Featured groups included a color grading community reported at $50,000 a month in 20 days from YouTube traffic, a dog psychology training group at $12,000 a month in 20 days, and Chris Bumstead's community at $24,000 a month from a single Instagram story.

## Why it matters

For anyone evaluating a Skool community, a closed group is now clearly marked rather than disguised behind a fake price. For owners, these tools protect Discovery stats and should lift trial and freemium conversion, which feeds Growth Boost.
