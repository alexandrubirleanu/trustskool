# Skool's First $1 Million a Month Community, Native Language Video Captions and Platinum Interviews

Skool News #72 celebrates the first creator in Skool's history to reach $1,000,000 a month in recurring revenue, then covers a Growth Boost restructuring, the full reviews rollout, native-language video captions and transcripts, and newly public interviews with $100,000-a-month creators.

## A new record: $1,000,000 a month

Logan (@logansendle) became the first person ever on Skool to hit $1 million in monthly recurring revenue, roughly $12 million a year. His community, Blue Collar Biz Accelerator, helps trades businesses such as landscaping, hardscaping, lawn care and deck building run more profitably. It costs about $1,600 a month.

## Growth Boost reset

Skool warned owners to expect 24 to 48 hours of turbulence in Skool network and Growth Boost traffic. The team is restructuring its campaigns across multiple ad accounts because of how many ads and campaigns it now runs. If traffic drops in that window, do not panic or change lots of settings; it should come back stronger.

They also noted that if you raised your bid on the new Growth Boost slider, the system only fully registers it once a transaction happens at the new percentage. After that, things move quickly.

## Reviews are live for all paid communities

After reviews beat no reviews in testing, they are now on for every paid community. Groups with zero reviews show nothing, to avoid hurting new groups. Only paying customers can leave reviews.

New moderation tools let any user report a review from its three-dot menu. Skool also auto-flags reviews and has a team checking them. A one-star "how do I cancel" post is a support request and can be removed, but a legitimate negative review will not necessarily be deleted just because it is reported.

A small change made a noticeable difference. Reviews could be left from the about page, from prompts in the feed, or from membership settings. Most inaccurate negative reviews came from membership settings, so that entry point was removed.

Two improvements are still coming. The "paying member since" label is not always accurate, for example when someone leaves and rejoins, or in freemium groups where someone was a free member long before paying. It will soon count only months actually paid. The label turns green at three months, because most churn happens in the first three months and reviews from longer-term payers carry more weight.

## Video captions and transcripts in your language

With Skool now in 11 languages, videos uploaded in Spanish, German or another language had still been getting English captions and transcripts. Now Skool auto-detects the spoken language and generates captions and a copyable transcript in that language. This only applies to new uploads, though re-uploading an older video would work.

## Roadmap

Reviews and languages are essentially done. Global fast payouts are rolling out, with India able to receive payouts in about a minute after previously having none, and currency conversion described as better than Wise. The new system largely decouples Skool from Stripe. Growth Boost optimization is the main focus, followed by payment methods like PayPal and Brazil's Pix, and local currencies such as euros and pounds.

## $100,000-a-month Platinum Interviews

Interviews recorded for Skool's private Platinum group are now public in the Skoolers classroom. Going platinum means making $100,000 a month or more. Four were available, including Oscar Patel at about $280,000 a month in health, plus creators in AI, trading and cybersecurity. Interviewees were picked from Skool's own data on who converts and retains best.

## Growth Boost wins and Skool of the Week

One SEO creator with a large YouTube channel saw daily about page visitors rise from about 300 to 3,200. The one-month edition of Skool of the Week featured groups built from scratch, including an AI SEO community at $51,000 a month within 30 days.

## Why it matters

A $1 million a month community resets the ceiling for what Skool groups can earn. Platform-wide verified reviews and more accurate tenure labels also give prospective members better signals when comparing communities.
