# Raising Skilled Readers Review 2026: Free Parent Education Community

What happens when reading practice at home turns into a nightly battle? That's the exact problem Raising Skilled Readers positions itself against. It's a free Skool community with 8,135 members, built around helping parents raise confident readers "without battles and uncertainty," through what it describes as expert support and parent education.

## What's Actually Inside

The description points to two components: expert support and parent education, both aimed specifically at reading skill development in children. That framing suggests the community isn't just a forum for swapping tips, but is structured around some form of guided instruction for parents, likely covering how to identify where a child is struggling, how to make practice sessions less stressful, and how to build reading skill over time rather than through pressure. The phrase "without battles and uncertainty" also signals that emotional dynamics around reading time, frustration, resistance, self-doubt as a parent, are treated as part of the problem being solved, not just the mechanics of phonics or vocabulary.

## Who Runs It

Doug Evans is the owner of this community on Skool.

## Pros

- Free access to parent education on a specific, high-stakes topic that many families struggle with.
- Addresses the emotional side of reading practice, not just technique, which matters for households where reading time has become a source of conflict.
- A large, active community size (over 8,100 members) suggests this resonates with a real, ongoing need among parents.

## Cons

- The description doesn't specify age ranges, reading levels, or the format of the "expert support," live sessions, courses, or downloadable materials, so it's hard to know exactly what a new member receives.
- No independent reviews were found beyond the platform listing to confirm outcomes for children's reading progress.
- Parents dealing with a diagnosed learning difference like dyslexia may need specialized support beyond what a general parent-education community can offer.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Parents of early or struggling readers who want practical, less stressful strategies for reading practice at home, and who are looking for both technique and emotional support rather than a purely academic curriculum.

## Who Should Skip It

Parents seeking a formal, diagnostic reading intervention program for a child with an identified learning disability should consult a specialist rather than relying on a general parent-education community. Parents of confident, already fluent readers may also find the content unnecessary.
