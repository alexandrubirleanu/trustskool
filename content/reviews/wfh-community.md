# The Official WFH Club Review: Help Landing a Remote Job for $47 a Month

Remote job boards are crowded, and scams are common, so a club promising legitimate roles is making a pointed claim. The Official WFH Club has 2,624 members, costs $47 per month, and says members get access to a team of 7 experts to help land the next remote role.

## What's Actually Inside

The description is mostly one promise: a team of seven experts who help you find a legitimate remote job. The listing does not name the experts, their fields or what the help looks like, whether it is CV reviews, job leads, interview practice or something else. So we can only describe the offer, not rate it.

The price is $47 per month in USD. Since job searches usually last weeks or months, the real cost is the subscription multiplied by how long you stay. The dataset also lists a 40 percent affiliate rate, which means members promoting the club may earn commission, a reason to read testimonials with some care.

Member numbers have crept upward in the latest snapshots, from 2,615 to 2,624, which suggests steady if modest intake.

Before joining, ask what happens if you do not land a role, whether jobs are vetted, and how quickly the experts respond. A promise of access is not the same as a promise of outcomes.

## Who Runs It

Cheska Lopez is the listed owner. The data shows she posts regularly, but it provides no background, so we will not describe her experience or the experts. Look for names, roles and examples of the help given.

## Pros

- Specific outcome focus: remote employment.
- Access to multiple experts is a clear selling point if it is real.
- Owner shows regular platform activity.

## Cons

- The experts are not named in the listing.
- $47 per month adds up over a long job search.
- No stated results or refund terms in the data.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Job seekers who want guided help with applications and are comfortable paying monthly during an active search. It suits people tired of sorting through doubtful listings alone.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone expecting a guaranteed job, people with a limited budget who can use free job boards and resume guides, and those who prefer to see credentials before paying. Cancel when your search ends so you do not keep paying.
