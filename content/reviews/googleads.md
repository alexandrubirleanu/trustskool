# Google Ads Foundations Review 2026: A Good Free Starting Point for PPC?

Google Ads can be one of the fastest ways to get customers, and one of the fastest ways to burn through a budget. Google Ads Foundations positions itself on the right side of that line. Its description is a single sentence: "Learn to profitably grow your business with Google Ads!"

The community has 1,940 members, and the listing shows no price, so it appears to be free.

## What's Actually Inside

The name and the description together give a clear picture of the intent, if not the details. "Foundations" signals an introductory level, and the word "profitably" suggests the focus is on return on ad spend rather than just getting clicks.

For a foundations-level Google Ads program, you might expect topics such as account structure, keyword research, match types, writing ads, conversion tracking, and budget management. To be clear, though, the description does not list any of these. It also does not say whether the community offers a course, templates, account reviews, live Q&A, or general discussion.

The phrasing "grow your business" suggests it is aimed at business owners running their own ads, rather than agency marketers managing many clients, but that is an inference rather than something stated.

## Who Runs It

The listing names Michael Alberse as the owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review does not comment on his advertising experience or certifications.

## Pros

- Free to join based on the listing.
- A specific, practical topic: Google Ads.
- Emphasis on profitability, which is the right mindset for paid advertising.
- A "foundations" level is approachable for people who have never run ads.

## Cons

- The description is one sentence with no curriculum or format details.
- No indication of whether advanced topics such as Performance Max, Shopping campaigns, or scaling are covered.
- No information about the owner's background or results.
- Unclear whether the free community is the full offer or an entry point to paid services.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Small business owners who want to try Google Ads themselves instead of hiring an agency right away, or who tried it once, lost money, and want to understand what went wrong. It also suits marketers new to paid search who want a basic grounding. Since it is free, it is a low risk way to see whether the teaching style works for you before spending money on a course or on ads.

## Who Should Skip It

Experienced PPC specialists will probably find a foundations-level community too basic. Anyone mainly interested in other ad platforms such as Meta, TikTok, or LinkedIn should look elsewhere, since this one is specifically about Google. And remember that learning in a community does not remove the cost of testing: running Google Ads still requires a real ad budget, and results depend heavily on your offer and market.
