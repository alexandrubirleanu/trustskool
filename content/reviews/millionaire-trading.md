# Millionaire Trading Review 2026: Does This Free Community Live Up to Its Name?

"Welcome to Millionaire Trading, where we turn gamblers into traders!" That's the community's own opening line, and it's worth pausing on before anything else: it's an unusually candid admission that many people arrive at trading communities already gambling, and a bold promise that this free, nearly 6,928-member group will change that.

## What's Actually Inside

Beyond that headline framing, the public description is thin: members are invited to introduce themselves and "get started" with the community, with a "let's win together" tone that leans more motivational than technical. There's no mention in the description of a specific trading methodology, asset class (stocks, options, forex, crypto), risk management framework, or track record, so it's not possible to say from the listing alone what "turning gamblers into traders" actually looks like in practice.

## Who Runs It

Camille van Merrienboer is listed as the community's owner on Skool. No further public information about trading credentials, licensing, or performance history is available here, so no claims about expertise can be made beyond that name.

## Pros

- Free to join, so there's no upfront cost to see what the community actually offers.
- The "gamblers into traders" framing at least signals awareness of a real problem, impulsive, unstructured trading, that many beginners fall into.
- A large membership base suggests an active space for discussion, even if quality is unverified.

## Cons

- Trading communities carry genuine financial risk, and a name like "Millionaire Trading" paired with vague promotional language is a pattern worth treating with real caution rather than excitement.
- No risk-management framework, credentials, regulatory disclosures, or performance data are named in the description, which is a notable gap for anything touching real money.
- "Turn gamblers into traders" is a catchy line, but without a documented method behind it, there's no way to verify whether the community actually reduces gambling-style behavior or simply reframes it.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Curious beginners who want a free, no-commitment first look at a trading community, provided they go in treating any trading discussion as educational at best and verify everything independently before risking real money.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for licensed financial advice, a documented and regulated trading strategy, or anyone who already struggles with impulsive or gambling-adjacent trading behavior should be especially cautious here and consider speaking with a licensed financial professional instead.
