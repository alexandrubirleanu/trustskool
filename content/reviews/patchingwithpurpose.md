# Patch With Purpose Review 2026: A Customer Support Space for Patch Users

Patch With Purpose is an English-language Skool community with 4,974 members, and its description is unusually direct about who it is for. It calls itself "the central community for patch customers to support, educate, and encourage one another throughout their patch journey." That wording points to a customer community built around a product, not a general course or coaching program. No paid price is listed in the data, so it appears to be free to join.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names three activities: support, education and encouragement, all happening between customers. That suggests a peer-driven space where people who already use patches compare experiences, ask questions and cheer each other on. It does not describe a structured curriculum, a classroom, or expert-led training.

What the description does not say matters just as much. It never names the patch brand or the type of patch, so a reader cannot tell from the listing whether this is about wellness patches, a supplement-style product, or something else. It also does not say whether the community is run by the company selling the product, an independent seller, or a group of users. The listed category is self-improvement, which is a broad label and not much help.

With 4,974 members, there is enough scale for questions to get answered and for discussions to exist at most hours. Whether the conversations are active or mostly announcements is something the member count alone cannot show.

## Who Runs It

The community is listed under the owner name Kerri Roberson. The data does not say whether she is a customer, a seller or a representative of a company, so check the about page before assuming any particular relationship to the product.

## Pros

- Clear purpose: a gathering place for people who already use the product, not a vague self-help promise.
- No paid price listed, so trying it should not cost anything.
- Peer encouragement can help people stay consistent with a routine they are trying to stick to.
- A sizable membership of 4,974 gives new users a good chance of finding someone with a similar question.

## Cons

- The product is never named in the description, so you cannot judge relevance before joining.
- Customer communities tied to a product can lean promotional, and the description does not clarify the owner's commercial relationship.
- Peer advice is not professional guidance. Nothing in the data suggests medical or expert oversight.
- No curriculum or lesson structure is described, so expect conversation rather than teaching.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Existing patch customers who want a place to swap experiences, ask practical questions and find motivation from others on the same path. It also suits people who prefer informal peer support over a formal course.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone not already using the product, or hoping for a structured program or expert instruction. If you want health or wellness advice you can rely on, treat any community like this as a supplement to professional guidance, not a replacement. Also skip it if you dislike product-centered groups where discussion might steer toward purchases.
