# Strength Therapy Review 2026: Does This Women's Strength Coaching Group Earn Its $34 Monthly Fee?

## What's Actually Inside

The listing uses the word "coaching," which sets a higher expectation than a simple discussion group. Three outcomes are named:

- Building muscle
- Building confidence
- Building sustainable habits

The anti-cardio, anti-quick-fix stance is the clearest signal of philosophy. It suggests the program centers on resistance training and long-term behavior change, rather than calorie restriction or short challenges.

What is missing is the format. The listing does not say whether coaching means individual feedback, group calls, written programs, video workouts, form reviews, or nutrition guidance. The listing shows $34 per month (USD). That sits in the middle range for paid fitness communities. Whether it is good value depends heavily on how much direct coaching is involved, since "coaching" can mean anything from personal check-ins to pre-written programs shared with everyone.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Women who want to start or get more serious about lifting and are looking for a sustainable approach rather than another short-term challenge. It is likely to appeal to someone who has done plenty of cardio without the results they wanted and wants to focus on muscle and strength instead. Women who value a supportive, women-only style of environment may also find this attractive, though the listing does not explicitly say membership is restricted.

## Who Should Skip It

If you want rapid weight loss or an aggressive diet plan, the stated philosophy runs against that. People who already follow a solid strength program and just want free community chat may not need to pay a monthly fee. Anyone with injuries, pregnancy, or medical considerations should confirm with a professional first, and ask the community directly how training is adapted.
