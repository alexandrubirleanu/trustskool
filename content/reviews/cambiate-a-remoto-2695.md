Breaking into a remote job from zero experience is a crowded promise online, and Cambiate a Remoto [CAR] makes it in Spanish, for free, to a community that has grown past 5,000 members. The description is short: "De cero a tu primer trabajo remoto: aprende, conecta y alcanza tu meta con apoyo real." From zero to your first remote job: learn, connect, and reach your goal with real support.

## What's Actually Inside

The pitch centers on three verbs, aprende (learn), conecta (connect), and alcanza (achieve), plus the phrase "apoyo real" (real support), suggesting the community combines some kind of learning content with peer networking and direct support toward the specific goal of landing a first remote job. The category tag is "tech," which points toward remote tech roles rather than remote work broadly, though the description itself doesn't name specific roles, whether that's customer support, software development, virtual assistance, or something else within tech. Members going in should expect to fill in those specifics themselves once inside.

## Who Runs It

Dani Conde is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here.

## Pros

- Free to join, which matters for a community explicitly aimed at people trying to break into their first remote role, often before they have income from that role.
- Over 5,000 members gives a reasonably sized peer network for people at a similar stage of the same job search.
- The "apoyo real" (real support) framing, combined with a "connect" pillar, suggests active peer interaction rather than a passive content library.

## Cons

- The description doesn't specify which remote tech roles or skills are targeted, so fit isn't clear without joining and looking around.
- No independent reviews of this specific community were found beyond its own Skool listing, so outcomes like actual job placement rates aren't verifiable here.
- "Zero to remote job" promises are common in Spanish-language online education, and this listing doesn't differentiate its method from competitors.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Spanish speakers starting from little or no experience who want a free, peer-supported path toward a first remote tech job and are comfortable filling in specifics once inside.

## Who Should Skip It

Non-Spanish speakers won't get much use from a community operating entirely in Spanish, and anyone already established in a remote tech career is likely past the "cero" (zero) starting point this community targets.
