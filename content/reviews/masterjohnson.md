# Mastering The Millionaire Review 2026: $997 One-Time Coaching Community for Established Coaches

Price is the first thing to understand here. Mastering The Millionaire charges a single payment of $997, not a monthly fee, and has about 3,880 members. The description is aimed at established coaches and consultants and is written with heavy emphasis: teaching them to master their mind and align with "#MILLIONS," and scaling a business from 10k to 80k+ with "The Mastery System."

## What's Actually Inside

The listing names one framework, The Mastery System, and two themes: mindset and business scaling. The 10k to 80k+ figure is not labelled as monthly or annual, nor as revenue or profit, and no evidence of anyone reaching it is provided, so it should be read as the owner's marketing claim. The listing does not say how the system is delivered, whether it includes calls, coaching, modules or templates, or how long access lasts. Those questions are worth asking before paying nearly a thousand dollars.

It is worth separating the two halves of the pitch. Mindset coaching and business scaling can overlap, but they are different things, and the description does not say how much of the program is each. The scaling range of 10k to 80k+ is also missing units and a timeframe, so it is unclear whether it is monthly revenue, annual revenue or something else. Without those, it does not tell you what a typical result would look like.

## Who Runs It

Brandon Johnson is listed as the owner. The data includes no further background or credentials, so we do not describe any.

## Pros

- A one-time payment means no ongoing subscription.
- The target audience is clearly stated: established coaches and consultants, not complete beginners.
- A named system gives you something concrete to ask about before you buy.

## Cons

- At $997, the cost is high for a listing with so little detail on content.
- The revenue figures lack context and proof.
- Mindset-focused language can be hard to evaluate. The listing does not separate practical tactics from motivational themes.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Coaches and consultants who already have clients and revenue, are comfortable with a mindset-led approach to growth, and are willing to ask the owner for specifics before committing the fee.

## Who Should Skip It

People who are just starting out, or who want to see a curriculum and outcomes before paying, should be cautious. Anyone who prefers tactical, metrics-driven training over mindset framing may find the pitch a mismatch.

If you are seriously considering the fee, ask for a refund policy in writing, a description of what happens after payment, and examples of client outcomes with context. A one-time price removes subscription risk, but it also means your money is committed upfront.
