# The Screenwriters Social Review 2026: Is $5/Month Fair for a Casual Story Space?

The Screenwriters Social has 4,677 members and charges $5 per month. The description keeps expectations modest: a casual space for creatives, film lovers, and writers to talk story. That framing places it closer to a hangout than a formal program, which is useful to know before paying.

## What's Actually Inside

The stated purpose is conversation. "Talk story" covers discussing plots, characters, films, and writing, and the audience includes film lovers who may not write at all. That mix means the community probably welcomes casual participants as well as working or aspiring screenwriters.

The description does not mention a curriculum, feedback sessions, script reviews, industry contacts, or contests. Those are features many screenwriting groups advertise, and their absence here is informative: do not assume them. If you want script notes or writing deadlines, ask whether that happens, and how, before subscribing.

At $5 a month, the commitment is small, so the question is less about value for money and more about whether you will use it. A cheap membership you never open still costs about $60 a year.

## Who Runs It

The listed owner is Shannan Johnson. The data does not include a biography or screenwriting credits, so none is claimed. If you want to know what experience she brings, look at her introduction in the community.

## Pros

- Low monthly price of $5, making it an easy experiment.
- A welcoming, casual tone that suits people who are nervous about sharing creative work.
- Open to both writers and film lovers, so conversations can be broad and varied.
- 4,677 members offers a sizeable pool of people with similar interests.

## Cons

- The description promises talk, not training. No structured learning is mentioned.
- No mention of script feedback or industry connections.
- Casual spaces can lose momentum if members stop posting, so check recent activity.
- A paid price for conversation may feel unnecessary when free writing forums exist.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Hobbyist writers, film fans, and aspiring screenwriters who want regular, friendly conversation about stories and are happy to pay a small amount for a curated space. It suits people who enjoy discussion more than formal instruction.

## Who Should Skip It

Writers who want rigorous critique, a structured screenwriting course, or industry networking. Those on a tight budget who can find similar discussion in free groups may also prefer to skip it. And anyone who needs a clear route toward selling a script should look for a program that advertises one.
