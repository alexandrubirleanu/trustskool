# The New Creator Review: A Free AI Community for Intentional Makers

Not every AI community is about shortcuts. The New Creator calls itself "a movement for creators, entrepreneurs, and media makers who use AI with intention" to build, create and lead. The English-language group has 2,501 members and no listed price.

## What's Actually Inside

The audience is wide: creators, entrepreneurs and media makers. The theme is AI used deliberately instead of for volume. The description does not name tools, courses, challenges or events, so we leave those out.

"Movement" and "lead boldly" are the community's own phrasing, and they suggest a values-driven tone more than a technical one. Whether that translates into useful lessons is something only the classroom can show.

If you join, look for examples: finished projects, workflows that members share, and discussion about when not to use AI. Those signs separate a working studio from a motivational feed.

Because the pitch is values-led, test it with a concrete project. Ask what you could make in a weekend with the resources inside, and see whether members share the process behind their work. A large following around the owner suggests a built-in audience, but what matters is whether lessons and feedback help you ship. With 2,501 members, there should be room for collaboration, so watch for partner requests and shared projects.

## Who Runs It

Juan Galan is the listed owner. The data records a large following and many Skool contributions, but no professional history, so we do not add one. Review his content to see whether his taste in creative work matches yours.

## Pros

- Free according to the listing.
- Values-led angle on AI use rather than hype.
- Broad enough for different kinds of creators.

## Cons

- Little concrete detail on what is taught.
- Audience is so broad that content may be general.
- Owner posting frequency looks modest lately.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Writers, designers, video makers and small business founders who want to adopt AI while keeping their own voice.

## Who Should Skip It

People looking for deep technical AI training, step-by-step tool tutorials, or a narrow niche. If you dislike mission-style language, this may feel vague.

Pick one project you want to finish and see whether the community helps you finish it. That is a better test than browsing posts. Treat the member count as context only, because engagement and the quality of conversation say far more about value than size does.
