# Curso Alimentación Infantil Review 2026: A Spanish-Language Course on Child Feeding

Parents of picky or struggling eaters tend to search in their own language, and Curso Alimentación Infantil is built for Spanish speakers. It has 3,489 members on Skool, which is a solid audience for a topic as specific as feeding children.

The one-line description reads, in translation: learn to assess feeding challenges in children with a clear method that can be applied from the first session. That is the entire public pitch we have to work with.

## What's Actually Inside

Two details in that sentence deserve attention. The first is the verb "evaluar" (to assess). It suggests the material teaches a way of looking at a feeding problem before reaching for a fix, which is different from a recipe collection or a meal-plan library. The second is "desde la primera sesión" (from the first session), which implies the content is organised in sessions and aims for early practical use.

What the description does not say matters just as much. It does not state the ages covered, whether the method is clinical, behavioural or nutritional, whether live calls exist, or what professional background sits behind the method. Treat any assumption about those points as unconfirmed.

## Who Runs It

The community lists Josefina Pizarro as its owner on Skool. Beyond that attribution, no verified details about background, training or credentials are available here, so none are claimed. Anyone considering a course about children's feeding should look for that information on the community page before paying or enrolling.

## Pros

- A Spanish-language offer in a topic where many resources are written in English first.
- A sizeable membership of 3,489 for a narrow subject, which suggests real demand.
- The "assess first" framing points to a structured method instead of loose tips.

## Cons

- The price data attached to this listing looks unusual and could not be treated as reliable, so no figure is quoted in this review. Confirm the actual cost, and whether it is a one-time payment or recurring, directly on the community page.
- The description gives no information on the author's qualifications, the age range, or the content format.
- Child feeding touches health. A community course cannot replace an assessment by a pediatrician or a registered dietitian, and nothing here should be read as medical guidance.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Spanish-speaking parents or caregivers who want a structured way to think about feeding difficulties and who plan to read the sales page closely, check the instructor's background, and confirm the price before committing. It may also suit people who already work with families and want to compare approaches.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone who needs a diagnosis, or whose child shows signs of poor growth, choking, allergic reactions or severe food refusal, should go to a medical professional first rather than a community course. Readers who need material in another language should also skip it, since the listing is written in Spanish and the content is presumably delivered in Spanish too. Finally, if the real price turns out to be far above what you expected, the thin public description gives little to justify the cost, so walk away until you have seen more evidence of what is included.
