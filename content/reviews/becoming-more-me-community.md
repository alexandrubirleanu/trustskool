# Becoming More Me Review 2026: Is This a Good Free Space for ADHD Entrepreneurs Fighting Procrastination?

Productivity advice written for neurotypical brains often fails people with ADHD. Becoming More Me is built for that gap, and specifically for entrepreneurs. The listing shows 1,957 members, an English-language audience, and no price, so it appears to be free.

Its description packs a lot into a few phrases: taming self-sabotage and procrastination, creating "relaxed success," and improving focus and follow-through, through nervous system and subconscious rewiring.

## What's Actually Inside

The audience and the problem are both clearly stated, which is more than many listings manage. The approach is also named: working with the nervous system and the subconscious, rather than relying on planners, apps, or willpower.

The phrase "relaxed success" stands out. It suggests the community is less about pushing harder and more about reducing the stress and internal resistance that often drive procrastination.

What the listing does not explain is how the rewiring work is done. It could involve guided practices, somatic exercises, coaching calls, journaling, or something else. There is no mention of courses, live sessions, or a schedule. It also does not say whether members need a formal ADHD diagnosis.

## Who Runs It

Theresa Lear Levine is the listed owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review does not comment on her professional training or her experience with ADHD or nervous system work. Since the approach touches on mental health territory, checking her background independently is sensible.

## Pros

- Free, based on the listing
- Very specific audience: entrepreneurs with ADHD
- Addresses root patterns like self-sabotage, not just time management tips
- Gentle framing ("relaxed success") that may suit people burned out by hustle culture
- Nearly 2,000 members who share similar challenges

## Cons

- The method behind "nervous system and subconscious rewiring" is not described
- No information on the owner's qualifications
- Unclear how much structure there is, which matters for an audience that often struggles with self-directed programs
- Not a substitute for clinical ADHD assessment or treatment

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Entrepreneurs and business owners with ADHD, or who strongly identify with ADHD traits, who keep sabotaging their own progress and are tired of productivity systems that never stick. If you are drawn to body-based or subconscious approaches and want to explore them with people who understand the entrepreneurial side of the struggle, this is a low-risk place to start.

## Who Should Skip It

People who want evidence-based ADHD treatment, medication guidance, or a formal diagnosis should work with a qualified clinician. Those who prefer concrete tactics, such as specific scheduling systems or business operations frameworks, may find a nervous-system-focused approach too abstract. And if you are not running a business, the entrepreneur angle may make some of the content less relevant.
