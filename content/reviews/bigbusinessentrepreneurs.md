# Big Business Entrepreneurs Review 2026: Free Support Network for Business Owners

Is a "support network" enough of an offer to justify joining a Skool community? Big Business Entrepreneurs, run by Adam Stott and home to 21,589 members, is pitched as exactly that: an exclusive space built to empower entrepreneurs and business owners, with no cost stated anywhere in the listing.

## The Pitch, As Written

The description is brief: "An exclusive support network designed to empower entrepreneurs and business owners." That names a clear audience, people already running or building a business, but stops short of describing format. There's no mention of live calls, courses, templates, events, or any specific deliverable. "Exclusive" is asserted rather than explained; the listing doesn't say what makes access exclusive when the community already lists over 21,000 members and no price at all.

That gap matters for anyone comparing this to other business-focused Skool groups. Communities in this space often differentiate themselves through named frameworks, guest experts, or structured accountability systems. Big Business Entrepreneurs doesn't lean on any of that in its public description, it leans on the word "network" itself, suggesting the value proposition is peer connection more than curriculum.

## Pros

- Free to join, which removes financial risk from checking it out.
- A clearly named audience, entrepreneurs and business owners, means less noise than communities aimed at "everyone."
- A member base above 21,000 suggests an active, established group rather than a ghost town.

## Cons

- "Support network" is vague on mechanism: no courses, calls, or resources are named in the description, so it's unclear what members actually receive day to day.
- The word "exclusive" isn't backed by any stated entry criteria or vetting process in the listing.
- There's no detail on Adam Stott's specific role, background, or what he personally contributes beyond being attached to the group's name.
- We found no independent reviews of this community outside its own Skool listing.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Established or aspiring entrepreneurs who want a free space to network with other business owners and don't need a structured curriculum to get value from membership.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for step-by-step training, a defined framework, or hands-on coaching should look elsewhere first and confirm what's actually delivered, since the listing describes a network rather than a program.
