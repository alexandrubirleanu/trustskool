# Must-Hire Engineers Review 2026: Can a Free Skool Group Really Get You Hired?

Most career communities stop at advice. Must-Hire Engineers goes a step further in its pitch: according to its description, members can learn the skills needed to become a "Must-Hire" engineer and get personally placed into a job through "Lanes network." That is a bold promise for a group that costs nothing to join, and it deserves a careful look before anyone treats it as a guaranteed route to employment.

The community currently lists 2,447 members on Skool and is free, with the description opening on a simple invitation to "Join the Free Group!"

## What's Actually Inside

The description names two things. The first is skill building, framed around what makes an engineer stand out enough to be considered a must-hire. The second is job placement, described as personal placement through a network associated with the owner.

What the description does not say is just as important. It does not specify which kind of engineering is covered, so software, mechanical, electrical and other disciplines are all possible readings. It also does not explain how placement works, who the hiring partners are, what the selection criteria might be, or whether every member is eligible. Without those details, the placement claim should be read as the community's own stated goal rather than a verified outcome.

## Who Runs It

Lane Mac is listed as the owner of the community. The description's reference to "Lanes network" appears to point to the same person, though the data does not confirm that link directly. No further background on Lane Mac is available in the data reviewed here, so this review makes no claims about their hiring history, industry connections or credentials.

## Pros

- Free to join, so the cost of checking whether the skills content is useful is only your time.
- A clear, specific goal: becoming more employable as an engineer, rather than vague self improvement.
- A membership of 2,447 gives some scale for peer discussion and comparing notes on job searches.

## Cons

- The engineering discipline is never named, which makes it hard to know in advance whether the material matches your field.
- Job placement is a strong claim, and the data offers no detail on how it works or how often it happens.
- Free groups that promise placement can sometimes serve as a funnel toward paid programs, and the description does not address whether anything paid exists.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Engineers early in their careers, or those actively job hunting, who want a no-cost place to sharpen their positioning and are curious about the networking side. It also suits anyone comfortable joining, exploring what is actually offered, and leaving if it does not match their discipline.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone who needs a guaranteed job outcome should not treat this as one, since placement details are not documented in the listing. Engineers looking for deep technical training in a specific discipline may also want to confirm the content matches their field before investing much time.
