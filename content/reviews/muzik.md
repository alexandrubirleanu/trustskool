# Müzik Üniversitesi Review 2026: Free Turkish Skool Community for Guitar and Singing

Learning an instrument usually means picking one platform and sticking with it, and Müzik Üniversitesi (Ücretsiz), a free Turkish-language Skool community, positions itself as a broader option: a place to learn guitar, singing, and other musical instruments. The name translates to "Music University (Free)," and the description is direct about scope: "Gitar, şarkı söyleme ve tüm müzik enstrümanlarını öğren," learn guitar, singing, and all musical instruments.

## What's Actually Inside

With nearly 8,930 members, this is a sizeable, free, Turkish-language hub covering multiple instruments rather than a single-instrument course. Guitar and singing get named specifically, alongside a broader claim to teach "all musical instruments," which likely means the community spans multiple instructional tracks or member-driven discussion across instrument types rather than one narrow curriculum. The listing doesn't detail lesson format, whether video courses, live sessions, or sheet music resources, nor does it specify skill levels covered.

## Who Runs It

Deniz Sayman is listed as the owner of this community on Skool.

## Pros

- Free access to instruction spanning multiple instruments, not just one.
- A large, active-sounding membership base for a Turkish-language music community.
- Guitar and singing are named explicitly, giving a clearer sense of core focus than the broader "all instruments" claim alone.

## Cons

- The description doesn't explain lesson format or structure, so it's unclear whether members get video courses, live coaching, or mostly self-directed resources.
- "All musical instruments" is a broad promise that's hard to verify from the listing; depth may vary a lot by instrument.
- The community is Turkish-language only, which limits access for non-Turkish speakers.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Turkish-speaking beginners or hobbyists who want a free, centralized place to start learning guitar, singing, or another instrument without paying for lessons upfront.

## Who Should Skip It

Non-Turkish speakers will hit a language barrier immediately. Advanced musicians looking for specialized, instrument-specific mastery content may also find a broad, multi-instrument community less useful than a dedicated teacher or narrower course. Anyone expecting a formal, conservatory-style curriculum with graded exams or certificates should also verify that expectation directly with the community, since the listing gives no indication of a formal credentialing structure.

Given that the community is free and covers two of the most commonly requested instruments, guitar and voice, alongside a broader promise of "all instruments," it's a reasonable low-risk starting point for Turkish-speaking beginners before committing money to a paid course elsewhere.
