# The $100K Sprint Society Review 2026: A $99/Month Community for Beginner Online Income

The $100K Sprint Society costs $99 per month and has 3,045 members. It teaches in English and is aimed at beginners. Its description: "We help beginners build online income on the path to their first $100,000 and beyond."

## What's Actually Inside

That sentence is about direction, not method. "Online income" could mean freelancing, e-commerce, content, affiliate work, agencies or any mix of them, and the listing names none. There is no syllabus, no tool list, no example of a member's progress, and no description of live calls or coaching. All the data reviewed here tells us is the promise: a path toward a first $100,000.

That figure is worth pausing on. "$100,000" is an aspiration used to frame the community, not a result the listing documents. It does not say how many members have reached it, how long it took, or what they did. Income in online business is highly uneven, and many people earn little or nothing, so the target should be read as marketing. A beginner-friendly program may still have genuine teaching value, but that is something to judge from the content, not the headline.

The price matters more than the pitch for most readers. At $99 monthly, a year of membership adds up to $1,188, which is well above what low-priced communities charge. Before paying, look for a refund or cancellation policy, a preview of the classroom, and trial or free-access options. Also ask whether the monthly fee covers everything or leads to further upsells.

With 3,045 members, the community has a reasonable base. That size can mean steady discussion, but the listing does not report engagement.

## Pros

- A beginner focus, which is useful for people who find advanced material overwhelming.
- A reasonable membership base of over three thousand people.
- Monthly billing means you are not locked into a long payment up front, assuming cancellation is simple.
- The goal is stated plainly, so you know the community's intended direction.

## Cons

- The $99 monthly price is high compared with many Skool communities, and the listing gives no content to justify it.
- The business model being taught is not named.
- The $100,000 target is aspirational and unsupported by documented member results.
- Nothing is said about coaching, calls or feedback, which usually separate a premium community from a library of videos.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Beginners who have already researched the community, seen what the classroom contains, and can afford the fee without strain. It also suits people who value group accountability and are realistic about timelines.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone on a tight budget, or hoping for a dependable route to six figures. Online income is uncertain, and a monthly fee should only be paid if you accept that you may earn nothing back. If you cannot see a clear curriculum before paying, consider waiting.
