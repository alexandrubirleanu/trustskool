# Gym Launch Review 2026: Can a Free Community Really Help Gym Owners Grow?

Running a gym is two jobs at once. You coach people, and you also have to keep the membership base growing, which is a sales and marketing problem most owners never trained for. Gym Launch is pitched squarely at that second job. The listing describes it as being "built for gym owners" and calls itself "the #1 gym growth community," with 2,188 members at the time of this review.

That "#1" claim comes from the description itself and is not something TrustSkool can verify. What the data does confirm is the size of the group and that there is no price attached to the listing.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names five areas: client acquisition, sales, content, automations, and a catch-all "& more." That is a sensible map of what a gym owner needs outside the training floor:

- **Client acquisition** likely means getting new leads and trial members through the door.
- **Sales** points to converting those leads into paying memberships.
- **Content** suggests social media or marketing material to keep the gym visible.
- **Automations** hints at follow-up systems so leads do not slip through the cracks.

The listing says all of this is available "for free." It does not describe the format, so it is unclear whether the material is a structured course, recorded trainings, live calls, or mostly discussion threads. It also does not say whether the free community leads into a paid program, which is a common pattern for growth-focused groups.

## Who Runs It

The listing names Chris Sachson as the owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review makes no claims about his gym experience, results, or credentials.

## Pricing

The listing shows no price, and the description explicitly says members can learn "for free." Both point the same way, so treat it as a free community.

## Pros

- Free to join, according to both the price field and the description.
- A narrow, clearly defined audience: gym owners, not fitness enthusiasts in general.
- Covers the business side of a gym (leads, sales, systems) rather than workout programming.
- A sizeable membership of over 2,000, so there should be peers to compare notes with.

## Cons

- The "#1 gym growth community" label is self-described and unverified.
- No detail on how the content is delivered or how deep it goes.
- No information on whether there is an upsell or paid tier behind the free access.
- Nothing in the data about the owner's background.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Owners of small or independent gyms, studios, and boutique fitness businesses who are comfortable coaching but feel stuck on marketing and sales. If you are trying to figure out how to fill classes, follow up with trial members, or set up simple automations, a free community focused on exactly those topics is a low risk place to start.

## Who Should Skip It

Personal trainers who do not own or plan to own a facility may find the focus too narrow. People looking for workout programs or nutrition coaching will not find that here, since the description is entirely about business growth. And if you already have a working acquisition and sales system, the free material may cover ground you know well.
