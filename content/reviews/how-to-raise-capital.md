# Capital Connectors Review 2026: Real Estate Capital Raising Community on Skool

Finding the deal is often the easy part of real estate. Finding the money is where many investors stall, and that is the problem Capital Connectors puts front and center. It has 2,722 members and bills itself as "the largest capital raising community on Skool."

## What's Actually Inside

The description is two sentences: "Raise capital for your real estate deals. Plug into the largest capital raising community on Skool to find more investors and do bigger deals." The pitch rests on two things, learning to raise capital and meeting investors.

Nothing in the listing explains how. It doesn't say whether there are training modules, investor directories, deal-pitching templates, live Q&As or just networking threads. "Largest" is a ranking claim we can't verify from the data, though the member count is substantial for a niche this specific.

## Who Runs It

Marcin Drozdz is listed as the owner. We have no further information about his background or deal history, so none is included here.

## Pros

- A single, clear purpose: raising money for real estate deals, rather than general investing talk.
- Networking with potential investors is built into the pitch, which can be hard to find elsewhere.
- No price is listed in the data reviewed, so the entry barrier may be low. Check the join page to be sure.
- Over 2,700 members indicates a meaningful pool of people active in this space.

## Cons

- The listing doesn't describe the content or format, so value is hard to judge before joining.
- "Largest" is unverified and says nothing about quality.
- Raising capital from investors can involve securities regulations, and the listing says nothing about legal guidance.
- We found no independent testimonials or evidence of deals funded.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Real estate investors, syndicators and aspiring sponsors who already have, or are building, a pipeline of deals and want to learn how others find investors. It suits people prepared to do their own legal and financial due diligence.

## Who Should Skip It

Complete beginners without any deal experience may find the topic premature. Anyone expecting investors to be handed over should be careful, as networking does not guarantee funding. And since fundraising can be regulated, nobody should treat a community as a replacement for a qualified attorney. Before sharing any deal with a stranger from a community, confirm who they are and what rules apply, and keep your own legal counsel in the loop.
