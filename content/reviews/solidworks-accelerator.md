# SOLIDWORKS Accelerator Review 2026: Can You Learn the Fundamentals in Two Weeks?

SOLIDWORKS is one of the most widely used 3D CAD programs in mechanical engineering and product design, and it has a reputation for a steep early learning curve. SOLIDWORKS Accelerator promises to flatten that curve. Its description is a single sentence: learn SOLIDWORKS fundamentals in just 14 days.

The community is free according to the listing, operates in English, and has 2,370 members.

## What's Actually Inside

There is not much in the description beyond the headline, but the headline is specific, and that counts for something. The focus is fundamentals, not advanced surfacing, simulation, or certification prep. The 14-day framing suggests a short, sequenced program, likely something a learner works through day by day, though the description does not confirm whether it is a video course, a series of exercises, a challenge format, or something else.

Fourteen days is an ambitious timeline. Getting comfortable with sketches, constraints, extrudes, and basic assemblies in two weeks is plausible for a motivated learner with regular practice time. Becoming job-ready is a different matter, and the description wisely does not claim that.

One practical point worth flagging: SOLIDWORKS itself is commercial software. The community being free does not mean the tool is free, and the description says nothing about whether members need their own license or how to get access to one.

## Who Runs It

Aryan Fallahi is listed as the owner. No further background on him is available in the data, so this review does not speculate about his engineering or teaching experience.

## Pros

- Free to join, which makes it a low-risk way to start learning a professional tool.
- A specific, time-bound goal that helps learners stay on track.
- Narrow focus on fundamentals, which is exactly where most beginners get stuck.

## Cons

- The description does not explain the format or how the 14 days are structured.
- Software access is a separate hurdle that the listing does not address.
- Fundamentals only, so anyone past the basics will likely outgrow it quickly.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Engineering students, makers, hobbyists, and career changers who want a structured, low-cost introduction to SOLIDWORKS. It may also help people who already use another CAD tool and want a quick on-ramp to SOLIDWORKS specifically.

## Who Should Skip It

Experienced SOLIDWORKS users looking for advanced techniques, simulation, or certification-level preparation. Also anyone who does not have, and cannot get, access to the software, since a fundamentals course is only useful if you can practice alongside it.
