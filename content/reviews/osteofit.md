# OSTEOFIT Review 2026: Is This Free Osteoporosis Community Right for Women Over 50?

OSTEOFIT has one of the most clearly defined audiences in this part of the TrustSkool directory: women over 50 living with osteoporosis. The community operates in Spanish, has 2,285 members, and is free according to the listing. Its description, translated, invites members to strengthen their bones and recover their vitality.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names two things directly: a free course and a community. That is more concrete than many listings, which often describe only a vague purpose.

The course content itself is not detailed. Given the promise to "strengthen your bones," it is reasonable to expect a physical activity or exercise angle, but the description does not say whether the material covers strength training, nutrition, posture, fall prevention, or something else. It also does not say how long the course is or how it is delivered.

The community side suggests a space where women dealing with the same diagnosis can share experiences and keep each other motivated. For a condition that often develops quietly and can feel isolating, that peer element may be as valuable as the course.

## Who Runs It

Javier Gimenez is listed as the owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review does not make assumptions about whether he is a physiotherapist, trainer, physician, or something else. That gap is worth keeping in mind for a health-focused community.

## Pros

- Free course plus community, with no subscription listed.
- A very specific audience, which usually makes content more relevant than general fitness programs.
- Spanish-language support for a health topic where quality resources in Spanish can be harder to find.
- A peer group of people facing the same condition.

## Cons

- The owner's qualifications are not stated anywhere in the data, which matters when the subject is a medical condition.
- Little detail about what the course covers or how safe exercises are adapted for different levels of bone loss.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Spanish-speaking women over 50 who have been diagnosed with osteoporosis or low bone density and want a free, supportive place to start working on their bone health. It may suit women who feel motivated by a group and want guidance that is specific to their stage of life rather than generic workout advice.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone who has not discussed their condition with a doctor should do that first, since exercise with osteoporosis needs to be appropriate to individual fracture risk. Women looking for medical treatment advice, medication guidance, or a clinical program should rely on their healthcare providers. And of course, people who do not speak Spanish, or who fall outside the stated audience, will likely find the material less relevant.
