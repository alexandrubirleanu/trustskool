# 3D Printing Skool Review 2026: Is $39 a Month Worth It to Design Your Own Products?

Owning a 3D printer and knowing how to design something worth printing are two very different skills. 3D Printing Skool targets the second one. Its description is a single sentence: learn how to make your own designs and products with 3D printing.

The community has 2,398 members and charges $39 USD per month, based on the listing. That's a meaningful recurring cost for a hobby, so it's worth being clear about what the data does and doesn't tell you.

## What's Actually Inside

The description centers on two outcomes: creating your own designs, and turning them into products. "Designs" points toward modeling and original creation rather than just downloading files from repositories, while "products" suggests at least some attention to making physical items people could use or sell.

Beyond that, the listing is thin. It doesn't name specific software, printer types, materials, or whether there's any guidance on selling what you make. There's also no mention of lesson format, live calls, or how structured the curriculum is. For a paid community, that's a fairly short pitch, and the details will need to be confirmed from the community's own about page or preview before subscribing.

## Who Runs It

Brandon Santana is listed as the community's owner. No further background is available in the data reviewed, so this page makes no claims about his experience with 3D printing or product design.

## Pros

- A clear focus on original design and product creation, which is the skill gap many printer owners hit.
- Membership of nearly 2,400 suggests a decent pool of fellow makers to learn from and troubleshoot with.
- Monthly billing means you can cancel if the content doesn't match your needs.

## Cons

- At $39 a month, costs add up quickly for a hobbyist, especially compared with free forums and tutorials.
- The description doesn't specify software, skill level, or curriculum, so it's hard to judge fit in advance.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Makers who already have access to a 3D printer and want to move beyond printing other people's files into designing their own objects. It may also appeal to anyone exploring the idea of producing physical products, as long as they're willing to pay a recurring fee for a guided environment and a community of peers.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone on a tight budget, or someone who just wants to print existing models, is probably better served by free resources first. If you need a specific CAD program or printer workflow covered, check that it's addressed before paying, since the listing doesn't say.
