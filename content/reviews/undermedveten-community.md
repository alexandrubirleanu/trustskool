# UM Mental Community Review 2026: Sweden's Self-Development Group on Skool

"Sweden's largest community for personal development" is a bold line, and it is exactly how UM Mental Community introduces itself. The group is Swedish-language, counts 2,880 members, and frames its purpose as helping people become "a more conscious version" of themselves.

## What's Actually Inside

The listing, written in Swedish, translates as: "Welcome to Sweden's largest community for personal development, a place to become a more conscious version of yourself! 🧘" The meditation emoji hints at a mindfulness flavour, though the text itself never spells out the methods.

That is all the description offers. There is no list of courses, challenges, live events or resources. We can't confirm the "largest in Sweden" claim from the data we have, so read it as the owner's positioning. The slug includes "undermedveten," the Swedish word for subconscious, which suggests an interest in inner work, but that is an observation about the name and not a described feature.

## Who Runs It

Tex Svanberg is listed as the owner. No other biographical details are available in the data, and we won't add any.

## Pros

- A Swedish-language space for personal development is a fairly specific offer, as much of this content is in English.
- No listed price in the data reviewed, so there seems to be no stated cost to look around. Verify on the join page.
- Nearly 2,900 members suggests a reasonably active audience for the theme.

## Cons

- The description is very short, so you can't tell what activities or structure to expect.
- "Largest in Sweden" is unverified.
- Personal development communities vary a lot in quality, and we found no independent feedback on this one.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Swedish speakers who want a community setting for reflection, mindfulness and self-improvement, and who prefer to explore a place and see whether the culture suits them. It works for people who like discussion and shared motivation more than strict curricula.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone who doesn't read Swedish will be shut out, since the listing and presumably the discussion are in that language. People wanting a precise, step-by-step program with measurable milestones may find the loose description unconvincing. And if you are dealing with significant mental health difficulties, a general self-development group is not a replacement for qualified professional support. If you try it, notice whether the posts are practical and kind in tone, since the culture of a self-development group matters as much as its size.
