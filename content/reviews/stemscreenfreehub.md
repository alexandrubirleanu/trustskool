# STEM & Screen-Free Hub Review 2026: A Good Alternative to Screen Time for Kids?

STEM & Screen-Free Hub is a free Skool community of 8,489 members aimed at families looking for hands-on alternatives to screen time, built around what its own description calls "fun STEM projects, 3D printing ideas, and simple hands-on challenges to help kids learn and play."

## What's Actually Inside

Based on the description, the content spans three related areas: general STEM projects, 3D printing specifically, and simpler hands-on challenges suited to younger kids or families without specialized equipment. That combination suggests a resource hub parents can pull from depending on their child's age and what tools they have access to, rather than a single age-locked curriculum. The "screen-free" framing positions it as an alternative activity source rather than an academic tutoring program.

## Who Runs It

Katie Miller is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or teaching credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review sticks to that basic attribution.

## Pros

- Free to join, letting families sample STEM and 3D-printing project ideas before investing in any paid curriculum or equipment.
- A specific, practical focus, hands-on projects and challenges, rather than generic "screen time reduction" advice with no actual activities.
- Over 8,400 members suggests a genuinely active base of parents swapping project ideas.
- Mentioning 3D printing specifically signals content for families who already have or are considering a 3D printer, a fairly specific niche within kids' STEM content.

## Cons

- The description doesn't indicate age ranges for the projects, so parents of very young kids or teens may need to filter content themselves.
- 3D printing projects assume some access to a printer or willingness to acquire one, which isn't a universal household setup.
- No independent reviews of project quality or safety vetting were found here, so parents should review activities themselves before handing tools to kids.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Parents looking for structured, hands-on STEM and 3D-printing project ideas as a screen-time alternative, particularly families already interested in maker-style activities.

## Who Should Skip It

Families without any interest in 3D printing or physical craft-style projects may find part of the content irrelevant to them. Parents seeking a formal, sequenced STEM curriculum tied to school standards should also look for a more academically structured program instead.
