# STEM & Screen-Free Hub Review 2026: A Free Family Community for Hands-On Learning

Not every Skool community is about making money, and STEM & Screen-Free Hub is a good example. It is a free English-language community of 8,489 members aimed at families, offering fun STEM projects, 3D printing ideas and simple hands-on challenges that help kids learn and play away from screens. Parents and educators who are looking for activity ideas are the obvious audience.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names three kinds of content: STEM projects, 3D printing ideas, and simple hands-on challenges. The word simple matters, because it suggests the activities are meant to be doable at home without specialist equipment, though the listing does not confirm what materials are needed. It also does not state the age range, how projects are organized, or how often new ones are posted. The screen-free framing is interesting alongside 3D printing, since printers are usually controlled from a computer, so some parts of the community may involve a screen at the planning stage.

## Who Runs It

The community is listed under Katie Miller. We have no verified details about her background or qualifications, so we say nothing further about them.

## Pros

- Free to join, which makes it easy for families to try.
- A clear, family-friendly purpose that is easy to understand.
- A mix of low-tech challenges and 3D printing ideas gives some variety.
- Nearly 8,500 members suggests a decent pool for sharing ideas and results.

## Cons

- The age range and skill level are not specified, so it may be hard to know whether projects fit your child.
- No curriculum or learning outcomes are described, and the educational value is the community's own claim.
- 3D printing ideas require access to a printer, which many families do not have.
- We found no independent reviews to confirm quality or safety guidance.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Parents, homeschoolers and teachers who want a steady supply of simple project ideas to keep kids busy and curious without a tablet in hand, and families who already own or are considering a 3D printer.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for a formal, graded STEM curriculum with assessments will find this too informal. Families without any interest in 3D printing may find part of the content irrelevant. Parents who need explicit safety guidance for projects should confirm what is covered before relying on it.
