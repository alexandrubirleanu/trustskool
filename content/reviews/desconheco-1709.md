# Desconheço Review 2026: Can a Free Portuguese Community Really Help Your Finances?

Money conversations tend to work better in your own language and with people who share your context. That seems to be the bet behind Desconheço, a Portuguese-language Skool community with 2,210 members. Translated, its description presents it as a community of Portuguese people who want to improve their financial life and help one another, closing with a patriotic "Viva Portugal!"

The listing shows no price, so it is treated here as free.

## What's Actually Inside

The description is short and focused on purpose rather than features. It tells us three things:

1. The topic is personal finance, framed broadly as "improving your financial life."
2. The audience is specifically Portuguese, which points to Portugal rather than the wider Portuguese-speaking world.
3. Mutual help is central. Members are expected to support each other, not only consume content.

What it does not tell us matters just as much. There is no mention of a course, a classroom, live calls, a curriculum, or a particular method. We do not know whether the focus is on budgeting, saving, investing, debt, side income, or taxes. Given the Portugal angle, it would make sense for discussions to touch on local banks, savings products, and the realities of Portuguese salaries and costs, but the listing does not confirm any of that.

With over two thousand members, the peer discussion side should at least have some activity, though member counts alone do not show how engaged people are.

## Who Runs It

The owner field lists João Pombo, described in that same field as a co-founder of Desconheço. No further background is available in the data, so this review makes no assumptions about his qualifications in finance or investing. If you plan to act on any financial advice shared inside, it is worth asking who is giving it and on what basis.

## Pros

- Free to join, according to the listing.
- Portuguese-language discussion with a Portugal-specific audience, which can make money topics more relevant than generic English content.
- A community-first, mutual-help framing rather than a hard sales pitch.
- A sizeable membership of 2,210 people for peer conversation.

## Cons

- The description gives almost no detail on structure, content, or format.
- No stated credentials for whoever leads the discussions, which matters for financial topics.
- "Improve your financial life" is broad, so members wanting depth in one area may need to look elsewhere.
- Peer advice in finance communities can vary a lot in quality.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Portuguese people who want a free, informal place to talk about money with others in a similar situation. It suits beginners who are looking for motivation, shared experiences, and a sense of community while getting their finances in order, and who are comfortable reading and writing in Portuguese.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone who needs structured financial education or professional advice on taxes, investments, or debt should look for qualified sources. People who do not read Portuguese, or who live outside Portugal and want locally relevant guidance for their own country, will probably get less out of it. And if you want a defined program with lessons and milestones, the listing gives no sign that one exists.
