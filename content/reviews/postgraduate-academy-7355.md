# PostGraduate Academy Review 2026: Is This Free Arabic Medical Training Community Worth It?

Medical education rarely gets the community treatment, but PostGraduate Academy has built a free, Arabic-language Skool space around exactly that gap, what happens to a doctor's training in the years after graduation. With 5,862 members and no listed price, its own description bills it as أول اكاديمية للاطباء, "the first academy for doctors," aimed at building an educational system that closes the postgraduate training gap.

## What's Actually Inside

The description is short but pointed. It states the goal is to bring together practical training (التدريب العملي) and clinical skills (المهارات السريرية) so members leave able to practice as a competent, confident doctor (طبيب كفء واثق). That framing suggests the community sits somewhere between a study group and a clinical mentorship space for physicians who have finished medical school but still need structured, hands-on guidance before or during residency-level work.

What the description doesn't say is just as notable: there's no mention of a specific specialty, no indication of whether content is delivered as live sessions, recorded lectures, or case discussions, and no reference to any formal certification or accreditation tied to the program. The Skool listing also has no category assigned, so there's nothing beyond the description itself and the 5,862-member count to gauge how the community actually runs day to day.

## Who Runs It

Ahmed Omer Elrayah is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review sticks to that basic attribution rather than speculating about his medical training or career.

## Pros

- Free to join, which matters for early-career doctors who are often the most financially stretched.
- A specific, stated mission (closing the postgraduate training gap) rather than a generic "medical community" pitch.
- A member base of nearly 5,900 suggests real, sustained demand among Arabic-speaking doctors for this kind of support.
- Explicitly pairs practical training with clinical skills rather than focusing on theory alone.

## Cons

- No specialty, format, or certification details are given, so prospective members can't tell what a typical week or module looks like from the listing alone.
- No independent reviews of this community were found beyond its own Skool page.
- The absence of a Skool category makes it harder to compare against similarly positioned medical education communities.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Arabic-speaking doctors in the early postgraduate stage who want structured peer support and practical clinical guidance beyond what medical school alone provided, and who are comfortable joining a free community with limited public detail on its exact curriculum.

## Who Should Skip It

Non-Arabic speakers won't get much use out of a community built and described entirely in Arabic. Doctors looking for training in one narrow specialty, or anyone who needs to see an accredited curriculum before joining, should reach out and confirm those details directly rather than assuming they're covered.
