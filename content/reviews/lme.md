# Let's Master English Review 2026: Free American English Speaking & Listening Practice

Let's Master English is a free Skool community with 7,051 members built around a specific, no-nonsense pitch: adults mastering American English speaking and listening, delivered seriously rather than through "cute" gamified lessons. Shane Peterson is named as the person running it.

## What's Actually Inside

The description draws a clear line under two skills, speaking and listening, rather than trying to cover reading, writing, grammar, and vocabulary all at once. It specifically claims over 100 live 1-on-1 sessions every month, a concrete, checkable volume claim rather than a vague promise of "personalized coaching." The phrase "no cute lessons, serious" signals a tone aimed at adult learners who want practical fluency rather than a playful app-style experience. Free lessons are also called out directly, suggesting there's a no-cost entry point even if paid options exist elsewhere.

## Pros

- States a specific, high-volume feature, 100+ live 1-on-1 sessions monthly, rather than a vague claim of personal attention.
- Narrow focus on speaking and listening specifically, useful for learners who've already covered grammar basics elsewhere and want conversational fluency.
- Free to join, with free lessons named directly in the description.
- Targets adult learners specifically, rather than a mixed-age, generic language-learning crowd.

## Cons

The description doesn't explain how the 100+ monthly 1-on-1 sessions are staffed, whether that's Shane Peterson personally, a team of coaches, or peer practice partners, which matters a lot for consistency of instruction quality. It's also unclear what, if anything, is gated behind a paid tier versus what's included free, and the listing doesn't say how session slots are booked or how quickly they fill given the size of the community. We found no independent reviews of this community beyond its own Skool listing.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Adult English learners who already have some base level and want serious, high-frequency speaking and listening practice specifically, rather than beginner grammar instruction or a gamified app experience.

## Who Should Skip It

Complete beginners looking for foundational grammar and vocabulary instruction may find this too narrowly focused on speaking and listening. Learners who prefer a lighter, more playful learning style should also note the description's explicit "no cute lessons" stance, and anyone hoping for written feedback on essays or exams should look elsewhere given the speaking-and-listening focus.
