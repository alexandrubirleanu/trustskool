# Let's Master English Review 2026: Free Skool Community for American English Speaking Practice

"No cute lessons. Serious." That's how Let's Master English describes itself, a free Skool community of 7,051 members built specifically for adults working on American English speaking and listening skills.

## What's Actually Inside

The headline feature here is scale of live practice: over 100 live 1-on-1 sessions offered monthly. That's a specific, checkable commitment, not a vague promise of "conversation practice," and it suggests the community is structured around real-time speaking with feedback rather than passive video lessons or worksheets. The description also draws a clear line between this and lighter language-learning content, calling out "no cute lessons" to signal a more disciplined, adult-focused approach to American English specifically, as opposed to British or generic English. Free lessons are explicitly available too, so members aren't required to pay just to get started.

## Who Runs It

Shane Peterson is listed as the owner of this community on Skool.

## Pros

- Over 100 live 1-on-1 sessions monthly is a concrete, high-volume offer that's unusual for a free language community.
- Explicitly targets adults, avoiding the mixed-age, gamified tone of many language apps.
- Focuses specifically on American English speaking and listening, a narrower and more useful positioning than generic "learn English" content.
- Free lessons are available, lowering the barrier for anyone wanting to test the format first.

## Cons

- The description doesn't say who leads the 1-on-1 sessions, native speakers, certified teachers, or volunteers, which matters a lot for accent and grammar coaching quality.
- No mention of scheduling logistics or time zones, which could matter for a global membership base of over 7,000.
- No independent reviews were found beyond the platform listing to confirm the "over 100 sessions monthly" figure holds up as the community scales.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Adult English learners who specifically want American pronunciation and listening practice through live, one-on-one interaction rather than self-paced apps or generic lesson libraries.

## Who Should Skip It

Learners who prefer British or other English variants, or who want structured written grammar instruction over live speaking practice, may find this community's format doesn't match their goals. Anyone uncomfortable with live 1-on-1 conversation, as opposed to group classes or recorded lessons, should look for a gentler starting point first. Beginners who need a slower, more forgiving pace than a "serious, no cute lessons" framing implies may also want to build basic vocabulary elsewhere before joining live sessions.
