# Digital Products Academy Review 2026: A Free Beginner Hub for Digital Product Businesses

Digital Products Academy calls itself the number one free community for beginners who want to launch simple, profitable digital product businesses without tech overwhelm. That ranking is the community's own claim and we cannot confirm it, but the numbers behind it are real enough to notice: 9,079 members, English-language, and no price attached. The description also says a full beginners course is inside, which is the main reason to look closer.

## What's Actually Inside

Two things are named directly: a beginners course and an approach built around simplicity. The phrase without tech overwhelm suggests the material tries to avoid complicated software stacks and keep the first product small. Digital products as a category can include templates, guides, printables and similar items, but the listing does not say which formats are taught, which platforms are used, or how marketing and sales are covered. The money category label fits, since the stated aim is a profitable business.

## Who Runs It

The community is run by Courtney Milam, according to the listing. We have no further verified details about her background or results, so we leave it there and suggest judging the teaching by what you see inside the free course.

## Pros

- Free, so there is no financial barrier for someone testing the idea of selling digital products.
- A full beginners course is promised, which gives new members a place to start instead of a bare feed.
- The simplicity angle suits people intimidated by software and setup.
- With over 9,000 members, there is likely to be an active pool of peers at a similar stage.

## Cons

- Number one is a self-description, not an independently verified ranking.
- The description does not list the specific product types, tools or sales channels covered.
- Free communities often exist partly to lead members toward paid offers, and the listing does not say whether that happens here.
- No outside reviews were found, so claims of profitability are unverified.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Complete beginners who want to explore digital products with no upfront spend and prefer a gentle, low-tech path. It also suits people who like learning alongside a large group of other newcomers.

## Who Should Skip It

Experienced sellers who already have products and a funnel will likely find the beginner framing too basic. Anyone who wants detailed advanced tactics on paid ads, scaling or software automation should look at more specialized options. If you are skeptical of any community that talks about profit without publishing evidence, treat the claims here with care.
