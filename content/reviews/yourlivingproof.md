# Family Addiction Support Review 2026: Free Strategies for Relatives of Addicts

Most addiction resources focus on the person using. Family Addiction Support turns the attention to the people around them. This Skool community has 4,870 members and describes itself as offering free strategies for spouses, parents and siblings of addicts, with the stated aim of helping members get the support they deserve and learn to help a loved one beat addiction. No paid price is listed in the data.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names three groups it serves (spouses, parents, siblings) and two goals: support for the family member, and effective ways to help the person struggling. "Strategies" suggests practical guidance rather than only peer chat, but the description does not say what form that takes, such as lessons, live calls, group discussion, or worksheets.

It is also silent on method. It does not say whether the approach follows a particular recovery philosophy, whether professionals are involved, or how the community moderates sensitive conversations. For a topic this personal, those are fair questions to ask before sharing your own story.

## Who Runs It

The listed owner is Emily Deaton. The data does not state her background, qualifications, or personal history, so it would be wrong to assume any. Check the community page for how she describes her own experience and whether any clinical credentials are claimed.

## Pros

- A clear and compassionate focus on family members, who are often overlooked in recovery conversations.
- Free to join, which removes a cost barrier at a stressful time.
- A large group of 4,870 members suggests many people in similar situations, which can reduce isolation.
- Covers several relationships (spouse, parent, sibling) rather than a single type of family.

## Cons

- The description does not mention professional or clinical oversight, so it should not be treated as medical or therapeutic advice.
- "Effectively help your loved one beat addiction" is a big promise. Outcomes depend on many factors outside any community's control.
- Content format and structure are not described, so quality is unknown until you look inside.
- Large, open communities on sensitive topics can vary in tone and moderation.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Family members who feel overwhelmed or alone and want a free place to learn practical approaches and connect with others in similar circumstances. It suits people who want peer understanding and structured ideas alongside, not instead of, professional help.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone in an emergency or a crisis such as overdose risk, who needs immediate professional or emergency services rather than an online community. People seeking treatment for themselves, rather than support as a relative, may be better served by a program built for the person with the addiction. And if you want clinically supervised family therapy, a Skool community is not a replacement.
