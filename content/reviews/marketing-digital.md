# Vive del Marketing Review 2026: Free Spanish-Language Digital Marketing Services Training

Selling marketing services is one of the most common freelance paths online, and Vive del Marketing speaks to Spanish-speaking beginners who want to try it. The Skool community has 3,194 members and no listed price.

The description, translated, says: learn to create and sell digital marketing services; I teach you a premium service and how to get your first client.

## What's Actually Inside

The promise has two halves. One is the service itself: a "premium service," singular, which the owner says he teaches. The other is acquisition: how to get a first client. Together that implies a narrow, productised offer plus an outreach or sales process, rather than a broad survey of every marketing channel.

What the description leaves out is the interesting part. It does not name the premium service, the type of client it targets, the format of the lessons, or whether there are live sessions or only recorded material. It also does not explain how a free community fits with the word "premium," which makes it fair to ask whether a paid upgrade or external offer exists. Nothing in the data confirms one either way, so check before assuming.

## Who Runs It

Nils Davila is listed as the owner on Skool. No further verified details about his experience or client results are available here, so none are stated.

## Pros

- Free to join, which lowers the risk of testing the material.
- A clear scope: one service and a first-client goal, instead of an overwhelming list of tactics.
- Delivered in Spanish, a language where practical agency-starter content is less abundant than in English.
- A membership of 3,194 suggests a reasonably active audience of beginners, though activity is not shown.

## Cons

- The service is not named, so you cannot judge relevance or market demand before joining.
- "Premium" and "free" sit awkwardly together, and the listing does not explain the business model.
- Landing a first client depends on market, effort and offer, and no community can promise it. Treat the stated aim as a goal, not an outcome.
- No verified evidence of student results appears in the data reviewed.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Spanish-speaking beginners who want a focused path into freelance or agency-style marketing and prefer to test a free resource before spending on courses. It also suits people who learn by following a single worked example more than by sampling many channels.

## Who Should Skip It

Experienced marketers who already sell services will probably find a first-client curriculum too basic. People who need teaching in English or another language should look elsewhere, as the content is aimed at Spanish speakers. If you want detailed, verified case studies before joining, the public description will not provide them. And if a free community that later pushes paid upsells would bother you, read the pinned posts and about page first so you know what to expect.
