# The Sacred Space Review 2026: Free Nervous System Community for High-Functioning Women?

With 6,612 members, The Sacred Space has built one of the larger free women's wellness communities on Skool, and it's positioned around a specific idea rather than generic self-care: nervous system regulation. Its own description is aimed at "high-functioning women ready to regulate their nervous system, reconnect with their bodies, and shift from overdrive to embodied power."

## What's Actually Inside

That one-line description is essentially all the public framing available. It doesn't spell out what the actual programming looks like, whether that's live calls, guided practices, courses, or purely peer discussion, so it's hard to say from the listing alone how structured the experience is. Nervous system regulation and somatic work are real, widely discussed areas of interest, but phrases like "regulate their nervous system" and "embodied power" are wellness language rather than a clinical description of a specific method, and shouldn't be read as a claim that any particular technique is medically proven.

## Who Runs It

The community lists Sammy Chessa as its owner. No further credentials, certifications, or clinical or coaching background are given, so this review sticks to that basic attribution rather than guessing at expertise.

## Pros

- Free to join, removing any financial barrier to trying it out.
- Speaks directly to a specific audience, women who feel stuck in "overdrive," rather than a broad wellness catch-all.
- A large membership (6,612) suggests real, sustained interest in the topic.

## Cons

- No detail on actual programming, format, or cadence is given publicly, so it's unclear what a typical week inside looks like.
- Terms like "regulate their nervous system" are marketing framing, not clinical claims, and the community shouldn't be treated as a substitute for therapy or medical care.
- No stated credentials for the founder specific to somatic or nervous system work.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Women who relate to the "overdrive to embodied power" framing and want a free community exploring nervous system regulation and body-based practices alongside others, as a complement to their own further research.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone managing diagnosed anxiety, trauma, or a nervous system condition should work with a licensed therapist or somatic practitioner rather than relying on a peer community as primary support. Those wanting a clearly structured curriculum, rather than an open, loosely defined space, may also want more detail before joining.
