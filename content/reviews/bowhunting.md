# The Ethical Predator Review 2026: Is This the Right Home for Traditional Bowhunters?

Most of the communities in the TrustSkool directory are about business, money, or fitness. The Ethical Predator is a refreshing exception. It is a community for traditional bowhunters, with 2,145 members and no price on the listing, which means it appears to be free to join.

The description calls it the "#1 Bowhunting community on SKOOL" and describes a global group of traditional bowhunters "who follow the code of traditional archery." The ranking claim comes from the community itself, but the audience is unusually well defined.

## What's Actually Inside

The listing is short, so it is easier to say what the community stands for than exactly what it contains. Three things come through clearly:

**Traditional, not modern.** The repeated emphasis on traditional archery suggests a focus on longbows, recurves, and similar equipment rather than compound bows with sights and release aids. That distinction matters a lot to people in this hobby.

**An ethical framing.** The name and the mention of a "code" point toward fair chase values, respect for the animal, and responsible shot selection. Expect discussion to lean into the why of hunting, not just the how.

**A global membership.** Because members come from different countries, conversations likely cover a range of game, terrain, and hunting regulations.

What is missing from the listing: any mention of courses, live calls, gear reviews, shooting form lessons, or a classroom. It may be a primarily discussion based community, but the data does not confirm either way.

## Who Runs It

Jenn Richardson is listed as the owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review does not assume anything about their hunting experience or role in the wider archery world.

## Pros

- Free to join, according to the listing.
- A very specific niche, so members share the same interests and values.
- An ethical focus that can help newer hunters build good habits.
- A global audience, which brings perspectives from many hunting cultures.
- Over 2,000 members is a sizable group for such a focused hobby.

## Cons

- No information about structured learning, so beginners may not find step by step instruction.
- Compound bow and crossbow hunters may feel out of place given the traditional focus.
- Hunting laws vary widely by country and region, so advice from members elsewhere may not apply to you.
- The "#1" claim is self described and not verified.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Hunters who shoot traditional bows, or want to make the switch, and are looking for people who share both their equipment choices and their values. It also suits archers who enjoy the craft side of the sport and want to talk with others who see bowhunting as a discipline rather than just a way to fill a tag. Because it is free, there is little downside to looking around.

## Who Should Skip It

If you hunt with a compound bow or rifle and have no interest in traditional gear, this group's focus may not match yours. People opposed to hunting will obviously find little here. And complete beginners who need formal safety instruction or licensing guidance should get that from a local hunter education course first.
