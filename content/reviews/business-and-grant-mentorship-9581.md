# Business & Grant Community Review 2026: Free Skool Group for Funding, Grants, and Credit?

Want access to business funding, grants, and personal credit-building tactics without paying to join a community first? That's the pitch behind Business & Grant Community, a free Skool group that has reached 7,135 members around exactly those themes.

## What's Actually Inside

The community's own description frames itself broadly: a community of entrepreneurs with access to "business funding, grants, boost your personal credit & more." That's a wide net. Funding, grants, and credit are each fairly distinct topics with their own mechanics, and the description doesn't break down which of the three gets the most attention inside, or walk through a specific process for applying for grants versus building credit versus securing other funding.

## Who Runs It

Jekwenta Primm is listed as the community's owner on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review sticks to that basic attribution rather than any claim about expertise.

## Pros

- Free to join, for topics, grants and business credit, that are often gated behind paid programs elsewhere.
- Covers several funding-adjacent topics (grants, credit, funding) in one place, useful for entrepreneurs who don't yet know which route fits their situation.
- A sizeable membership (over 7,100) suggests meaningful ongoing interest and activity around these topics.

## Cons

- The broad "grants, credit & more" framing means it's unclear how deep the community goes on any single topic, especially something as involved as grant applications.
- No independent reviews or outside data confirm actual grant approval rates, credit outcomes, or funding secured by members.
- Grant and business-credit spaces online sometimes mix legitimate guidance with questionable tactics, so members should independently verify any specific strategy before acting on it.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Entrepreneurs starting to explore funding options, grants, or business credit building, who want a free starting point and are willing to research and verify any specific tactic before relying on it. Newer founders who don't yet know the difference between a grant, a line of credit, and a funding round may benefit simply from exposure to the vocabulary and the questions to ask next.

## Who Should Skip It

Business owners who already have an established relationship with a lender, grant consultant, or credit advisor, and are looking for advanced or highly technical guidance, may find a broad free community less useful than specialized paid help.
