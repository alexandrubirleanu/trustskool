# HobbyScool Review 2026: Free Virtual Summits for Makers

HobbyScool takes a different approach than most Skool communities in this space: instead of a curriculum or coaching program, it's built around a recurring calendar of free events. The description promises "a full year of free events," with monthly virtual summits and seasonal retreats bringing makers together, and 7,035 members have joined so far at no cost.

## What's Actually Inside

The structure here is event-based rather than lesson-based. Every month brings a free virtual summit, and the calendar also includes seasonal retreats, suggesting a recurring rhythm rather than a one-time onboarding sequence. The audience is described as "makers," a term generally associated with hands-on crafting, building, and DIY-style hobbies, though the description doesn't name specific hobby categories like woodworking, sewing, or electronics.

## Who Runs It

Destini Copp is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review sticks to that basic attribution.

## Pros

- Completely free, with an explicit "full year of free events" commitment rather than a limited trial.
- Recurring monthly summits plus seasonal retreats give members a predictable cadence of content and connection.
- Event-based format may suit people who prefer live, community-driven learning over static video courses.
- A membership of over 7,000 suggests the event calendar has real staying power.

## Cons

- The description doesn't specify which hobbies or maker disciplines are covered, so fit depends heavily on the individual events scheduled.
- Members who prefer structured, self-paced courses may find a purely event-driven format less useful for deep skill-building.
- No independent reviews were available to confirm how consistently the monthly summit cadence has actually been kept.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Hobbyists and makers who enjoy live virtual events and want a free, recurring calendar of summits and retreats to stay connected with a maker community, particularly those who value community connection and variety over a single deep skill track.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for a structured, skill-specific curriculum in a particular hobby, rather than a general events calendar, may find the format too broad for focused learning. Members in time zones that make live virtual summits inconvenient to attend may also get considerably less overall value if recordings aren't reliably available afterward.
