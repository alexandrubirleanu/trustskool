# HobbyScool Review 2026: Is This Free Year-Round Maker Community Worth Joining?

With 7,035 members, HobbyScool has built a free Skool community around a specific promise: "a full year of free events, made for you," anchored by monthly virtual summits and seasonal retreats for makers.

## What's Actually Inside

Rather than a single course or curriculum, the description frames HobbyScool as an ongoing calendar of events, monthly virtual summits and seasonal retreats bringing "makers together," which suggests the core value is live participation and community connection tied to hobbies and craft rather than a static library of lessons. That event-driven model means the experience likely varies month to month depending on what's scheduled, rather than offering a consistent, linear path.

## Who Runs It

Destini Copp is listed as the community's owner on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review is limited to that basic attribution.

## Pros

- Free to join, with free virtual summits and retreats named directly in the description rather than gated behind a paywall.
- A recurring, year-round event structure, monthly summits and seasonal retreats, gives a concrete reason to keep checking back rather than a one-time resource dump.
- A sizeable membership (over 7,000) for a hobbies-focused community suggests healthy ongoing engagement.

## Cons

- "Makers" and "hobbies" are broad categories, and the description doesn't specify which crafts or hobby types the summits and retreats actually cover, so members interested in a specific niche should verify fit before assuming full alignment.
- No independent reviews or outside data on event turnout, quality, or what a typical seasonal retreat actually involves were available here.
- An events-driven format depends on live attendance for the most value, so members who can't regularly join live sessions may get less out of it than the description implies.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Hobbyists and makers who want a free, recurring calendar of virtual summits and retreats and enjoy connecting with other crafters live rather than working through a fixed self-paced course. Anyone who enjoys sampling a variety of crafts throughout the year, rather than committing to one, may especially appreciate the rotating format.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for a specific, single-skill deep-dive course, or members who can't attend live events regularly, may find a broad, event-driven hobby community less valuable than a focused paid program in their exact craft.
