# Global Digital Nomads Review 2026: A Free Network for Working Abroad

Global Digital Nomads describes itself as the number one free digital nomad community, built to network and to learn how to work abroad. It has 4,716 members, is in English, and the data shows no paid price, which agrees with the "FREE" in its description.

Unlike many Skool communities that sell a course, this one is framed mainly as a network. That shapes what you should expect from it.

## What's Actually Inside

Two activities are named: networking and learning. Networking implies conversations, introductions, and likely location-based or topic-based threads where nomads compare notes. Learning how to work abroad could cover remote income options, visas, destinations, and logistics, but the description lists none of these, so those are guesses, not facts.

The "number one" claim is a self-description. The description gives no basis for it, and with several nomad communities across the internet, it should be read as marketing. What members can reasonably check for themselves is activity: how often people post, whether questions get answers, and whether the content is current. Travel rules and visa programs change often, so older posts may be out of date.

## Who Runs It

The listed owner is Jay Larosa. The data includes no biography, so we do not describe his background. Look at his introduction and any posts about his own remote work to see how much first-hand experience he brings.

## Pros

- Free, with no paid price listed, so the cost to try it is low.
- A sizeable community of 4,716 members spread across what is by nature a global audience.
- Combines connection with learning, which suits a lifestyle where isolation is a common complaint.
- A focused topic that is easy to understand.

## Cons

- The learning content is not described, so it is unclear whether there is structured teaching or only conversation.
- "Number one" is unverified.
- Practical advice on visas, taxes, and legal matters can vary by country and change quickly, so always confirm with official sources.
- Free networking groups can be noisy, with self-promotion mixed in.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

People thinking about leaving a fixed location for remote work, and current nomads who want to meet others and swap tips. It fits those who value community over formal instruction.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for a step-by-step course on building remote income. Those who need authoritative legal or tax guidance should consult professionals, since a general community is not a reliable source. If you prefer small, private groups, a large open network may feel impersonal.
