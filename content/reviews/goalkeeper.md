# Total Goalkeeping Review 2026: Free Community for Pro Keepers and Scholarships

Few Skool communities name their outcome as precisely as Total Goalkeeping does: helping football goalkeepers "become professional keepers or gain a college scholarship." At 7,777 members and free to join, the community also claims a long track record, saying it has "been helping keepers and parents since 1998," well before Skool itself existed as a platform.

## What's Actually Inside

The description is unusually specific for a free community: it names a single position (goalkeeper), a single sport (football), and two concrete outcome paths, turning professional or earning a college scholarship. That points to a training and guidance resource aimed squarely at competitive-level development, rather than general fitness or recreational play. The "since 1998" claim suggests the group's methods, if accurate, predate the Skool platform and were likely ported over from an earlier coaching operation.

## Who Runs It

Ray Newland is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here, so this review sticks to that basic attribution.

## Pros

- Free membership for a specialized coaching niche that is often gatekept behind paid academies.
- Extremely specific target outcomes (turning pro, or earning a scholarship) rather than vague skill promises.
- A long claimed history (since 1998) suggests this isn't a brand-new venture testing the waters.
- Speaks directly to both keepers and parents, useful since youth athletes are often supported by family decision-making.

## Cons

- The "since 1998" claim isn't independently verified here, only stated in the group's own description.
- No specific training methodology, drills, or scouting connections are detailed in the listing itself.
- No independent reviews were found to confirm actual scholarship or pro-signing outcomes for members.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Competitive youth and amateur goalkeepers (and their parents) who are specifically pursuing college scholarships or a professional pathway, rather than casual players. It's also worth a look for coaches or club staff who want a free resource to point committed young goalkeepers toward, given the group's claimed multi-decade focus on this one position.

## Who Should Skip It

Outfield players, casual or recreational goalkeepers not pursuing competitive pathways, and anyone outside football entirely will find this too narrowly focused to be useful. Parents expecting guaranteed scholarship placement should also treat any such outcome as one possibility among many, not a promise.
