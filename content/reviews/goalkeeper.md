# Total Goalkeeping Review 2026: Free Community for Pro and College Keepers

Few Skool communities can point to a track record stretching back to 1998, but that's exactly the claim behind Total Goalkeeping, a free community now at 7,777 members. Its stated mission is narrow and clear: helping football goalkeepers become professional keepers or earn a college scholarship, with support extended to both keepers and their parents.

## What's Actually Inside

The description sets out two concrete outcome paths, turning pro or securing a college scholarship, which suggests the community's content is organized around competitive development rather than casual skill-building. Involving parents as a named audience alongside the keepers themselves points to guidance on the recruiting and scholarship process, not just on-field technique, since navigating college athletics recruitment is often as much a parent's task as a player's. The "since 1998" detail, if accurate, would put this among the longer-running goalkeeper-specific coaching operations to move onto Skool, though the description gives no further specifics on training methods, session formats, or which age groups are covered.

## Pros

- Free access to a niche coaching community focused specifically on goalkeepers, a position often underserved by general football training content.
- Dual outcome focus (professional path and college scholarship path) covers two of the main goals young keepers and their families actually have.
- Parents are explicitly included as part of the audience, acknowledging that scholarship and recruiting processes involve family, not just the athlete.
- A long claimed operating history (since 1998) suggests institutional experience, if the group's Skool presence reflects that same long-running program.

## Cons

- The description doesn't detail training methods, age ranges, or how coaching is delivered (video review, live sessions, written guides).
- The "since 1998" claim is self-reported, with no independent source cited to confirm continuous operation.
- No independent reviews were found to verify how many members have actually reached professional or college-scholarship outcomes through the community.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Youth and teen goalkeepers pursuing competitive or college-level football, and their parents who want guidance on both technical development and the scholarship or recruiting process.

## Who Should Skip It

Outfield players, or goalkeepers playing purely recreationally with no interest in competitive advancement, are unlikely to find the college and pro-focused content relevant to their goals. Families outside the college and pro pipeline entirely, such as those focused only on local recreational leagues, may also find the scholarship-oriented framing less useful.
