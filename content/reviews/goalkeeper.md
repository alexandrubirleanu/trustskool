# Total Goalkeeping Review 2026: Goalkeeper Development Since 1998

Total Goalkeeping says it has been helping keepers and parents since 1998. The community on Skool has 7,777 members, is free to join, and targets football goalkeepers hoping to turn professional or earn a college scholarship.

## What's Actually Inside

Two goals are named in the description: becoming a professional keeper and gaining a college scholarship. Both are demanding outcomes that depend on many factors, and the listing does not describe the training content, coaching format or scholarship guidance that would support them. The mention of parents is useful, since youth goalkeepers often rely on family for decisions about teams, camps and recruitment. The "since 1998" line is the community's own statement and was not independently checked. The data also lists an affiliate percentage of 40, which only indicates a referral arrangement.

For parents in particular, it is worth keeping expectations realistic. Only a small share of young players reach professional football, and scholarship routes depend on recruitment, academics, timing and luck as well as training. A community can help by sharing information and encouragement, but it cannot guarantee a result. A good test is whether the content talks honestly about development stages, rest, injury prevention and academic planning rather than only about the glamour of the end goal. The listing does not say, so read some posts before deciding how much weight to put on its advice.

Ray Newland is the listed owner. The data does not contain further biographical details, so none are claimed here beyond the community's own "since 1998" statement about its work.

## Pros

- Free to join.
- Addresses both keepers and their parents, which fits how youth sport actually works.
- Clear aspirational goals around professional and scholarship paths.
- A community of 7,777 members gives access to many keepers and families.

## Cons

- The description does not explain the training content or format.
- Professional contracts and scholarships depend heavily on factors beyond any community, and no outcomes are verified.
- The "since 1998" experience claim is self-reported.
- Free membership may be an entry to paid services, which the listing does not clarify.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Ambitious young goalkeepers and their parents who want a free place to learn about the path toward higher-level football and to connect with others pursuing it.

## Who Should Skip It

Outfield players are not the target. Anyone seeking guaranteed scholarship outcomes, or detailed technical coaching documented in advance, should skip it, as the listing offers neither.
