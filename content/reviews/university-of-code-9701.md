# University of Code Review 2026: Free Skool Community for Aspiring Developers

Free coding communities are common on Skool, but University of Code has grown to 5,444 members behind a simple promise: exclusive lessons, content, and what it calls "badass" community support for developers.

## What's Actually Inside

The description states: "You'll get Exclusive Lessons & Content, Badass Community Support & More here to elevate as a Dev!" That positions the community around a mix of content and peer support aimed at helping developers "elevate," but it doesn't specify which programming languages or frameworks are covered, what skill level the lessons target, beginner, intermediate, or advanced, or the delivery format, whether that's video courses, live sessions, or code reviews.

## Who Runs It

Sonny Sangha is listed as the owner of University of Code on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here.

## Pros

- Free access to lessons and community support for a technical skill often gate-kept behind paid bootcamps
- Combines content with community support rather than functioning as a passive course library
- A large community size, over 5,400 members, for peer learning and networking

## Cons

- The description doesn't specify which programming languages, frameworks, or skill level the lessons target
- No independent reviews were found beyond its own Skool listing
- "Exclusive Lessons & Content" is vague marketing language without concrete examples of curriculum or outcomes

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Aspiring or early-career developers who want free lessons alongside an active peer community, and who are comfortable exploring the specific curriculum firsthand before committing more time.

## Who Should Skip It

Experienced developers looking for advanced, specialized technical content, or anyone who needs to confirm a specific language or framework focus before joining, may want to look elsewhere or ask directly first.
