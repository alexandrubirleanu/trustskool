# 1% Club Review 2026: A Chinese-Language Growth and Business Community

The 1% Club, whose Chinese name plays on "billion" and "1 percent," is a Chinese-language Skool community with 6,980 members. It is listed as free. Translated, its description promises: improve a little every day and become one of the top 1% in your field, replace useless and hard-to-use traditional communities with your best private traffic pool, learn new business monetization strategies, and enjoy mind-body-spirit exchange.

## What's Actually Inside

There are four stated pillars: daily progress, a community that doubles as a private audience (a private traffic pool, meaning an audience you own), new ways to earn from your work, and wellbeing. The text ends with a note that making money is not all of life, and that the aim is overall improvement instead of a pure exchange of interests. The listing does not describe formats, courses or events, and it does not say how the community differs from others in practice. The mix of business and wellbeing is unusual for a Skool community and may appeal to people who find purely profit-driven groups tiring. The description explicitly says money is not the most important thing in life, which signals a community culture that values balance, though how that shows up in daily activity is not stated.

## Who Runs It

The owner is listed as Jerry Huang. The data holds no further biography, so none is added. The listing also shows an affiliate percentage of 40.

## Pros

- Free to join.
- Chinese-language content for a market with fewer Skool communities.
- A balanced message that places money alongside wellbeing.
- 6,980 members offers a decent pool for networking.

## Cons

- The description is a list of aspirations, and the specifics of what is taught are not given.
- The "top 1%" idea is a motivational slogan with no measurable meaning.
- There is no independent verification of monetization results.
- Non-Chinese speakers will not be able to use it.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Chinese speakers who want a mix of self-development and business ideas, and who like a community that treats money as one part of a bigger picture.

## Who Should Skip It

Readers seeking a strictly technical business course, or anyone who does not read Chinese. People wanting a precise syllabus should ask first.
