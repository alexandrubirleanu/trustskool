# The Barakah Collective Review 2026: A Free Home for Business Owners and Designers?

Business owners and designers often need each other, but they rarely end up in the same online spaces. The Barakah Collective tries to put them together. Its description calls it "a space for business owners and designers to connect, grow, share tips, and build brands that actually make an impact, together."

There is no income promise, no countdown, and no list of bonuses, just a collaborative pitch. The community is free and lists 2,279 members.

## What's Actually Inside

The description emphasizes four activities: connecting, growing, sharing tips, and building brands. The repeated focus on "together" suggests a peer-driven community where members learn from one another rather than a top-down course.

The mix of audiences is the most interesting part. A business owner might come for branding advice or to find a designer; a designer might come to understand clients better or to build their own design business. The phrase "brands that actually make an impact" hints at values-driven branding, though the listing does not define what impact means here.

The description does not explain the name "Barakah" or tie the community to any particular audience beyond business owners and designers, so this review does not assume more than that.

There is no mention of courses, live calls, critiques, or job boards.

## Who Runs It

Derya Akbayrak is listed as the owner. No further background is available in the data reviewed here, so this page makes no claims about her design or business work.

## Pros

- Free, with a friendly, collaborative tone and no hype.
- Brings business owners and designers into one space, which can create useful cross-perspective conversations.
- Branding focus is practical and relevant for small businesses.

## Cons

- No concrete details about content, structure, or events.
- "Make an impact" is appealing but not defined.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Small business owners who want to improve their branding, and freelance or in-house designers who want a supportive peer network with clients' perspectives in the mix. People who value community and collaboration over formal instruction should enjoy the format.

## Who Should Skip It

If you want a structured design course, portfolio reviews with set schedules, or guaranteed client leads, the listing promises none of those. People outside business and design will find little reason to join.
