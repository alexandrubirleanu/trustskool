# WFH Job Hunt Review 2026: Remote Job Leads Community With a Listed $35 Price

Searching for a work-from-home job can feel like sorting through noise, and WFH Job Hunt aims to cut some of that down. Its 2,915 members get what the listing describes as remote leads posted daily, plus resume support. The twist is on price: the description leans heavily on the word "free," yet the page lists a one-time price of $35.

## What's Actually Inside

The description opens with "Free Remote Leads Posted Daily" and goes on to say this is "Not Your Average Remote Community." It lists free remote resumes, resume tips and tricks, and support "& more."

So the visible offer is three things: daily job leads, resume help, and general support. We don't know what kinds of roles the leads cover, which countries they apply to, how they are sourced, or whether "remote resumes" means templates or reviews. The "& more" is not defined.

## Who Runs It

Destiny Casey is listed as the owner. We have no further information about her background and won't guess at it.

## Pros

- A practical, concrete promise: leads and resume help are things a job seeker can use right away.
- Daily posting, if maintained, keeps the feed fresh.
- The one-time structure of the listed price, rather than a recurring fee, is easier on a tight budget.
- Almost 2,900 members indicates real interest.

## Cons

- Free in the description versus $35 on the listing. We can't say which applies to which content, so check before paying.
- Job leads are only as good as their sourcing, and the listing says nothing about vetting for scams or quality.
- No independent feedback was found confirming that members actually land interviews or jobs.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

People actively hunting for remote or work-from-home roles who want a steady feed of leads and some guidance polishing a resume, and who will verify each opportunity themselves. It also works for anyone willing to sample the free-sounding parts before deciding on the fee.

## Who Should Skip It

Those who want career coaching tailored to one industry, or interview training in depth, may find the description too general. Job seekers who are uncomfortable with any pricing ambiguity should skip until the real cost is clear. And as with any lead feed, anyone unwilling to independently screen postings for legitimacy should be cautious.
