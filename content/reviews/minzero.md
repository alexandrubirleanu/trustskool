# Min Zero Review 2026: Can This Free Arabic Community Really Get You to $30K a Month?

Min Zero ("From Zero") is a free, Arabic-language Skool community that has reached 7,101 members around a promise aimed squarely at beginners: start with nothing and build a real project, with the description stating that even complete beginners can reach earnings "between $3,000 and $30,000 monthly."

## What's Actually Inside

The description welcomes members regardless of experience level and frames the community as a starting point for building an income-generating project from scratch, without naming a specific business model, niche, or industry. That vagueness matters. A $3,000 to $30,000 monthly income range is an enormous spread, and without a defined method (e-commerce, freelancing, content, something else) or any supporting data, it reads as a broad marketing hook rather than a documented, typical outcome for members.

## Who Runs It

Omar Moudni is listed as the community's owner on Skool. No independent background or track record is available in the data reviewed here, so this review is limited to that basic attribution.

## Pros

- Free to join, so there's no cost to see what the community's actual content looks like beyond the headline promise.
- Explicitly welcomes total beginners, which may appeal to people who feel priced out of more advanced paid programs.
- A sizeable membership (over 7,100) suggests real ongoing interest in the Arabic-speaking market for this kind of content.

## Cons

- The "$3,000 to $30,000 per month" income range is a wide, unverified claim with no case studies, business model, or methodology named in the description, and it should be treated skeptically rather than as a typical or likely result.
- No detail on what the actual project-building process involves, so prospective members can't evaluate the substance of the teaching before joining.
- Free communities built around big income claims often lead into a paid upsell, so members should expect that possibility rather than assume everything valuable stays free.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Arabic-speaking beginners curious about building an online project who want a free, low-risk way to explore the space, provided they treat the income figures as aspirational marketing rather than a guarantee.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone likely to make financial decisions, such as quitting a job or taking on debt, based on the stated income range should hold off, since no evidence backs that specific figure. Non-Arabic speakers will also get limited value from a community conducted in Arabic.
