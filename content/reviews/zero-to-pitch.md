# Zero to Pitch (Free) Review 2026: Can a Free Community Get Your Pitch Deck Investor Ready?

Most founders polish their pitch deck for weeks before anyone outside their team sees it. Zero to Pitch argues for the opposite approach. Its description says the sooner you start, the faster you will see what investors respond to, and it invites people to join free and begin building both a pitch deck and a spoken pitch.

The community has 2,154 members and, as the name says, costs nothing according to the listing.

## What's Actually Inside

The stated focus is narrow and practical: the pitch deck and the pitch itself. Those are two different skills. A deck is a document that has to make sense on its own when forwarded, while a pitch is a live performance that has to hold attention in a few minutes. It is a good sign that the description names both.

The "start sooner" framing suggests an iterative, learn by doing approach rather than a long theory course. That said, the listing does not describe the actual materials. We do not know whether there are deck templates, example decks, recorded lessons, live feedback sessions, or opportunities to pitch to other members. We also do not know which funding stage it targets, such as pre-seed, seed, or later rounds.

TrustSkool categorizes it under money and business, which fits the fundraising theme.

## Who Runs It

Brett Fox is the listed owner. No further background is available in the data, so this review does not assume anything about his investing, founding, or advisory experience. If you plan to rely on someone's view of what investors want, it is worth checking that background independently.

## Pros

- Free to join, so there is no cost to testing whether the approach suits you.
- A tight focus on one high-stakes task that founders often struggle with.
- Covers both the deck and the verbal pitch, not just slides.
- A member base of over two thousand, which could mean a pool of other founders for practice and peer feedback.

## Cons

- The description is brief and does not list specific resources or formats.
- No indication of which funding stage or industry the advice is tailored to.
- Free communities sometimes serve as an entry point to paid offers. The listing does not say whether that is the case here, but it is worth watching for.
- Feedback from peers is not the same as feedback from active investors, and the listing does not promise the latter.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

Early-stage founders who are preparing to raise money for the first time and want a structured nudge to stop overthinking and start drafting. It also suits people who learn best by producing something, getting reactions, and revising, and who want to practice delivery as well as design.

## Who Should Skip It

Founders who have already raised several rounds and have a proven deck may find the material too basic. Businesses that do not plan to seek outside investment, such as bootstrapped or lifestyle companies, will get little from a pitch-focused community. And anyone expecting introductions to investors should note that the listing does not mention any.
