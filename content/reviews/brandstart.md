# BrandStart™ Review 2026: A Free French Community for Getting Clients From Social Media

Views are easy to count, customers are harder. That is the whole argument of BrandStart™, a free French-language Skool community with 3,043 members. The description, in French, reads roughly: "Getting views is good. Getting clients thanks to social networks is better!!"

## What's Actually Inside

The listing separates two goals: audience attention and actual customers. By contrasting them, it signals a focus on conversion, meaning turning social content into inquiries and sales, instead of growing follower counts for their own sake. That is a useful distinction, since many creators have reach without a business model.

Beyond that headline, the description is silent. It does not name platforms (Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn), types of businesses served, content formats, or whether the lessons are aimed at freelancers, local businesses, or coaches. The trademark symbol in the name suggests a brand identity, but the listing does not say whether there is a larger paid program behind it.

When you visit, look for concrete material: example posts, offer-building frameworks, a clear process from content to conversation to client. Those would show the promise has substance.

## Who Runs It

Lucas Dampeyrou is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further verified information about background was available in the data reviewed, so this review does not add any.

## Pros

- Free, which lets you evaluate the approach at no risk.
- French-language, serving a market where much social media marketing teaching is in English.
- A clear, commercially minded focus on clients rather than vanity metrics.
- A membership of 3,043 is notable for a French-language Skool community and suggests an active audience.

## Cons

- The description is two short lines, with no detail about method, platforms, or target business types.
- Results from social media client acquisition depend heavily on offer, niche, and effort, so the listing cannot imply any particular outcome.
- A free community may primarily lead to paid coaching or courses, which should be checked.
- Exclamation-heavy phrasing is promotional rather than informative.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

French-speaking freelancers, consultants, and small business owners who already post online but struggle to translate attention into inquiries, and who want a community to compare approaches with. Browse a few posts to see whether advice is specific, with examples of content and offers, or purely motivational. Specifics are the better sign that the approach can be applied to your own situation.

## Who Should Skip It

Non-French speakers, as both the pitch and the community operate in French. Anyone who needs a guaranteed method, or who expects a fixed number of clients, should note that no such promise is made or could reasonably be made. People who want deep technical training in a single platform may prefer a specialist resource.
