# Airbnb Startup University Review 2026: Free 30-Day Arbitrage and Co-Hosting Plan

Thirty days is the timeline Airbnb Startup University puts on its own pitch: "Launching your Airbnb Arbitrage / Co-Hosting business in 30 days!" The Skool community behind that promise is free to join and had reached roughly 5,105 members at last count, sitting in Skool's money category.

## What's Actually Inside

The description names two specific business models directly: rental arbitrage, where someone leases a property and re-lists it on Airbnb, and co-hosting, managing an Airbnb listing on behalf of the actual property owner for a fee or split. Both are established models within the short-term rental space, and naming them specifically gives the pitch more concrete grounding than a vague "make money with Airbnb" claim would. The 30-day launch framing suggests a structured, sequential process rather than open-ended browsing, though the description doesn't detail what that 30-day path actually contains week by week, or whether it covers finding landlords willing to allow arbitrage, negotiating co-hosting agreements, or the operational side of guest management and cleaning logistics.

## Who Runs It

Adam Hager is listed as the owner of this community on Skool. No further independent background or credentials are available in the data reviewed here.

## Pros

- Free to join, which is notable given that arbitrage and co-hosting education is frequently sold as a paid course or coaching package elsewhere.
- Naming two specific, well-established business models, arbitrage and co-hosting, rather than a vague "passive income" pitch gives prospective members a clearer idea of what they're signing up to learn.
- A 30-day framing suggests a defined, time-bound path rather than an indefinite, unstructured learning process.

## Cons

- The description doesn't detail how landlord relationships, local regulations, or short-term rental legality are addressed, all of which vary significantly by city and are critical to arbitrage specifically.
- No independent reviews of this community were found beyond its own Skool listing, so the real-world success rate of the 30-day plan can't be verified here.
- Arbitrage and co-hosting businesses depend heavily on local market conditions and landlord or HOA rules, factors a general course description can't fully account for.

## Who It's a Good Fit For

People interested specifically in Airbnb arbitrage or co-hosting as a business model, who want a free, structured starting point and are willing to do additional local research on regulations and landlord agreements.

## Who Should Skip It

Anyone looking for guidance on owning and operating their own short-term rental property, rather than arbitrage or managing someone else's, should confirm the content applies before joining. People in markets with strict short-term rental regulations should also research local legality independently, since a general course can't account for every jurisdiction.
