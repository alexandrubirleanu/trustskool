## Process notes

- No external research (WebFetch/WebSearch) was used anywhere in this run. Every claim in every review comes only from the fields in `data/communities.json` (description, display_name, owner_name, price fields, total_members, language, category, activity/growth stats). No founder biography was invented for any community; where `owner_name` was present it was stated plainly with a note that no further background is available, matching the site's existing pattern for unverified owners.
- The covered-slug set was rebuilt from scratch by reading `community_slug`/`slug` frontmatter across all 2,981 files in `content/reviews/` and all 222 files in `content/founders/` (3,203 unique slugs total), not carried over from the previous run's log, to guarantee no double-writes even if the dataset or existing content changed shape between runs.
- Generic-language filtering (rule 2) was applied as a pre-filter pass before dispatching any writing work, based on manual review of all 100 candidate descriptions. This is a judgment call re-applied fresh each run; several of the largest candidates by member count keep failing it week over week since their descriptions haven't changed (see note above the skip list).
- Work was split across 7 parallel batches (six of 10 communities, one of 4), each independently instructed on tone, rules, and format with the exact same non-negotiable rules and two worked examples (`chatgpt.md`, `gohighlevel.md`) used as style references. After all batches completed, the full 64-file output was re-verified programmatically against every non-negotiable rule (word count math, em dash scan, CTA format, required sections, slug collisions) independently of the writing agents' self-reported checks; 6 files needed correction (2 stale word counts, 4 bodies expanded from the 393-399 range up into the 400-650 target) before all 64 passed clean.
